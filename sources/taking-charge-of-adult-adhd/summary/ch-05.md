# Chapter 5. What Will the Evaluation Tell You?
Pages 26–39.

1. **Main idea.** A feedback conference gives an opinion on ADHD or other problems plus recommendations (p.26). Diagnosis needs symptoms plus impairment relative to the norm, and Barkley argues his 9-item adult list beats the DSM-5's child-derived 18 items (pp.27–28). Accepting ("owning") the diagnosis is required for treatment commitment (p.37).
2. **Named method/routine.** Diagnostic criteria (p.26): high inattention and/or hyperactive-impulsive behavior; far more often than peers; ≥6 months; onset before 12–16; adverse consequences across domains in childhood and adulthood. Murphy's four evaluation questions (p.27). DSM-5: ≥5 symptoms on either 9-item list (p.27) vs Barkley: 4 suffices (pp.27–28). DSM-5 presentations: combined (~65%+), predominantly inattentive (~30%+), predominantly hyperactive (p.35). Time: not stated; the feedback conference usually ends the evaluation (p.26).
3. **Tools/containers.** None for personal organization.
4. **Notes/thoughts/reference.** Not addressed. Reference: ask for re-evaluation with the 9-item list if told you have fewer than 5 DSM symptoms (p.27).
5. **Transfers/review.** Not addressed.
6. **Worksheets/deadlines.** Tables: 18 DSM symptoms, adults with ADHD vs community (pp.33); 10 impairment domains (p.34). Second-opinion test: seek one only on "solid, rational grounds" (p.37). No deadline.
7. **Key terms.**
- *Impairment*: "the social and other adverse consequences or costs that result from expressing the symptoms of ADHD", defined relative to "the norm" (p.30).
- *Presentation*: DSM-5 subtype (hyperactive, inattentive, combined); Barkley says not stable "qualitatively different forms" (p.34).
- *Sluggish cognitive tempo (SCT)*: "a separate deficiency in a different type of attention"; 30–50% of apparent inattentive cases may be SCT (p.34). Not discussed further (p.36).
- *Comorbidity*: see ch.2.

Other claims: prevalence 5–8% children, 4–5% adults (p.37); adult male:female ~1.6:1 (p.37). Non-ADHD explanations: age >55/perimenopause, thyroid/sleep problems, heavy drug use, stress, brain injury (p.39). Adults with ADHD averaged >12 of 18 DSM symptoms vs <1 for community sample (p.32).
