# Chapter 20. Rule 5: Feel the Future
Pages 178–182.

1. **Main idea.** Rule 5 "keeps you motivated to reach your goals" (p.178). Motivation comes from emotion, so consciously imagine how it will feel to finish, because for adults with ADHD the threat of negative consequences is not a potent incentive (p.178) and the feeling must be generated by "a very conscious, deliberate effort" (p.181).
2. **Named method/routine.** Rule 5. Sequence: Visualize it → Verbalize it → Feel it (p.180). Steps: (a) find where you are least motivated—school, work, chores, community, relationships; which to-do items "grow old" (p.179); (b) ask yourself point blank, "What will it feel like when I get this done?" (p.181); (c) name the emotion (pride, self-satisfaction, happiness, accomplishment, others' pleasure) and "work hard to feel it, right then, right there" (p.181); (d) repeat "every day, or every time you are in that situation" and keep doing so "all the way, until you get there" (p.181); (e) cut out pictures of the rewards and place them around you while working (p.181); (f) if you can't imagine the feeling, recall a similar task you did finish (p.182). Time: not stated; repeated each work session (p.182).
3. **Tools/containers.** Pictures of the rewards, placed around the workspace: strengthen imagery and anticipated emotion (p.181). Mental store of past successes from Rule 2: supplies emotional material (p.182). No other tools.
4. **Notes/thoughts/reference.** Not addressed. Mentions your to-do list as a place where unmotivating items accumulate (p.179), but no handling rule.
5. **Transfers/review.** Not addressed; "Every time you sit down to continue working … you must … feel that future outcome" (p.182).
6. **Worksheets/deadlines.** Fill-in prompts: hardest-to-motivate areas; stale to-do items (p.179). No deadline.
7. **Key terms.** *Emotion as source of self-motivation*: "our emotions are the very source of our self-motivation" (p.180). Emotion-action pairs: fear → fight/flight; anger → right a wrong; joy → keep doing; shame → avoid repeating (p.180). *Begin with the end in mind* (Covey, cited, p.182). *Inner emotional well*: practice fills it (p.182).
