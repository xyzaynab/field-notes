# Chapter 25. Work
Pages 210–223.

1. **Main idea.** ADHD can hurt more at work than at school (p.211); success depends on the right mix of work, environment, procedures and people, plus accommodations, medication, tools, a coach/mentor, and allies (pp.211–223). Early studies of teen jobs missed this because those jobs were unskilled, part-time and temporary (p.211).
2. **Named method/routine.**
- **Fit checklist** (pp.211–219): type of work; working environment; work procedures; the people.
- **Coach/mentor routine at work** (pp.220–221): meet "twice a day for 5 minutes"—set goals in the first meeting, review what you accomplished in the second; thank with small tokens; keep to company business only (p.221).
- **E-mail/phone batching** (p.217): check e-mail briefly at the start of the day for urgent items, then at lunch and at the end of the day; check phone messages "two to three times per day (beginning, middle, end)"; keep browser closed; shut down the phone when concentrating.
- **Supervisor review meetings every 3–6 weeks** (p.223).
- **Walk before long meetings**: a few minutes' brisk walk improves concentration "for about an hour afterward" (p.222).
- **Interview questions** to ask (p.218): how long will X take; advance notice of projects; how often check in; report format; solo or team.
- **Disclosure** question: disclose if you will invoke ADA accommodations; otherwise optional (pp.219–220).
3. **Tools/containers, one job each.**
- **Day planners, smartphones, your journal, e-mail calendar**: "keep track of tasks, goals, deadlines, promises, appointments, and any other time-related information" (p.221). Journal: "I can't stress enough how critical your journal is to success in all domains of your life" (p.221).
- **MotivAider or smartphone vibrate timers (set or random)**: prompts to stay on task (p.221).
- **Smartpen / digital recorder**: record important meetings, with the supervisor's permission (p.221).
- **Clock app, calendar/e-mail alerts, phone alarm**: make passing hours visible when no clock or window (p.217).
- **Laptop/tablet with handwriting recognition**: take notes in meetings; "the sheer physical motion … will keep you focused" and gives a reference later (p.222).
- **Noise-canceling Bluetooth headphones with instrumental music**; closed office door; opaque-walled cubicle or private office (pp.216–217).
- **Storage space for files**: avoid hunting for materials (p.217).
- **Posted list of the eight rules** near the monitor (p.220).
- **Disability specialist in HR**: receives documentation, explains accommodations, refers to clinicians (p.221).
- **Workplace library/information center, after-hours sessions, volunteer seminars** (p.222).
4. **Notes/thoughts/reference.** Meeting content → typed or handwritten notes taken throughout, as a later reference (p.222). Meeting recordings → Smartpen/recorder (p.221). Reading before a meeting → SQ4R (p.222; method in ch.24 pp.206–207). Tasks, goals, deadlines, promises, appointments → planner/smartphone/journal/e-mail calendar (p.221). Learning-on-the-job material → company library, coworker tutoring (pp.222–223). Where meeting notes are stored afterward: not addressed.
5. **Transfers/review.** Journal and tools "to keep track of tasks…" with no stated transfer between them (p.221). Coach reviews daily (twice) (p.220); supervisor reviews every 3–6 weeks (p.223). Email checked three times a day (p.217).
6. **Worksheets/deadlines.** The yes/no environment questions (pp.216–217), procedures (p.218–219), people (p.219) work as a checklist. A list of ADHD-friendly occupations (pp.214–216): military; door-to-door/B2B sales; drug rep; skilled trades; EMT/ER doctor/police/firefighter; sports and sports-related jobs; computer technician; food service; self-employment/entrepreneurship; photographer/videographer; performing arts. No deadlines. Interview tip: ADHD adults interview worse than others; a coach can train you (p.213).
7. **Key terms.** *ADHD-friendly job*: freedom of movement, changing settings, immediate consequences, interaction, little long-range planning (pp.214–216). *Coach/mentor*: someone "you can make yourself accountable to every day" (p.220). *Fit as a mix*: "the right mix of the work you do … and the environment … Challenges in one can be offset by benefits in the other" (p.213).
