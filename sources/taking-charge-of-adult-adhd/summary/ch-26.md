# Chapter 26. Money
Pages 224–231.

1. **Main idea.** ADHD creates a "money pit everywhere you turn" (p.224): impulse spending, unpaid bills, no savings (p.224). Use the Step Four rules (mind's eye, voice, heart, playground) plus structural systems—automatic deposits, a third-party manager, a written budget—so that good intentions don't depend on in-the-moment self-control (pp.225–231). Barkley's practical recommendations, not individualized financial advice.
2. **Named method/routine.** "A New Approach to Money Management" (pp.228–230), in order: (1) use community resources/free credit counseling if in trouble—banks or credit unions may meet "monthly" to keep you accountable (p.228); (2) let a trusted non-ADHD spouse/partner/parent manage your money, for a preset period or goal, with automatic bill-pay (p.228); (3) **Budget**: monthly budget sheet listing all expenses, including one-twelfth of annual expenses; keep it visible on desk or dresser (pp.228–229); (4) live below 90% of monthly earnings, saving 10% (p.229); (5) enforced savings: 10% of pretax earnings into a retirement plan (e.g., 401(k)), direct-deposit paycheck, bank auto-moves 10% to savings monthly, plus an emergency account (p.229); (6) get health/disability insurance via employer (p.229); (7) balance your bank statement monthly "without fail" (p.229); (8) keep all receipts: nightly, empty wallet receipts into a file (p.230). Spending limits (p.230): cash basis; no ATM/credit card where possible, one card labeled "For Emergency Use Only"; avoid shopping with no real need; don't lend money; avoid casinos/online gaming; seek CBT for impulse buying. Credit (pp.230–231): don't borrow for consumables/entertainment; have a money manager review contracts; never use payday loans.
3. **Tools/containers, one job each.**
- **Photo of a long-term goal in your pocket**: pull out at the urge to buy (p.226).
- **Sticky note "NO!" on credit card**: stop impulse (p.226). **Rules card banded to the credit card**: can't use card without seeing rules (p.226).
- **Gatekeeper friend (call/text before purchases; reciprocal)**: accountability (p.226).
- **Alarm** for bill paying (p.226); **journal** for reminders and for recording spending to see patterns (pp.226–227).
- **Automatic payroll deposit and bank auto-transfer**: enforce saving (p.227, 229). **Automatic bill pay**: rent, mortgage, car loan (p.228).
- **Monthly budget sheet (pencil and paper preferred over software)**: show all obligations vs income (p.229).
- **Wallet + nightly receipt file**: record spending, support tax deductions (p.230).
- **Online bank mobile app**: keep track of balance (p.229).
4. **Notes/thoughts/reference.** Spending patterns → journal (p.227). Receipts → wallet, then a file nightly (p.230). Budget → on desk or dresser (p.229). Financial rules → spoken or written on a card with the credit card (p.226). Reference material on community/credit counseling resources: not addressed (see ch.19 for general reminder tools, p.226).
5. **Transfers/review.** Nightly: wallet receipts → file (p.230). Monthly: balance bank statement (p.229); budget reviewed "often" (p.229); credit counselor meets monthly (p.228). Wait: journal-to-budget transfer is not described.
6. **Worksheets/deadlines.** Research list of money problems (p.224). Rules of thumb: expenses under 90%, saving 10% (p.229). Minimum credit-card payment assumes a 10-year payoff (p.230). Start "today" for living within means (p.229).
7. **Key terms.** *Emotional spender* (p.226–227). *Gatekeeper* (p.226). *Enforced savings*: "The less you see of your cash, the less you can spend it impulsively" (p.229). *Payday loan*: "legalized loan sharking" (p.231).
