# Chapter 1. Is It Possible That You Have ADHD?
Pages 5–13.

1. **Main idea.** Adults with ADHD are "blind to time, or nearsighted toward the future": they "live in the 'now' and struggle to deal with all those 'nexts'" (ch.1 p.5). A self-screen (symptom checklists) helps you decide whether to seek a professional evaluation; only a professional can confirm (ch.1 pp.6, 10).
2. **Named method/system/routine.** No productivity routine. Screening steps, in order: (a) 14-item "How would you describe your problems?" yes/no list (p.5–6); (b) check history—did symptoms start in childhood, before age 16 (p.8, p.13); (c) 9-symptom adult checklist (p.10): ≥4 of the first 7, or 6 of all 9, = "highly likely" ADHD (p.10); (d) final 6-question "What's Next?" list (p.13). Time required: not stated.
3. **Tools/containers.** None recommended. (Pointer to Appendix list of 91 additional symptoms, pp.271–276, p.6.)
4. **Notes/thoughts/reference material.** Not addressed. Only suggestion near this topic: ask a parent or sibling about your childhood if you can't recall it (p.8).
5. **Transfers/review.** Not addressed.
6. **Worksheets/exercises/deadlines.** The checklists above. Criteria: symptoms ≥6 months (p.10); onset before 16 (98% of cases, p.8); impairment in ≥1 major domain (p.13). No decision deadline.
7. **Key terms (author's words).**
- *Dimension, not category*: "ADHD is not a category that you either fall into or don't. It is not like pregnancy. It's more like human height or intelligence." (p.11)
- *Symptoms vs. impairments*: "Symptoms are the ways a disorder expresses itself in thoughts and actions. Impairments are the adverse consequences that result from showing those symptoms." (p.11) Disorder begins "where impairment in a major life activity occurs." (p.11)
- *Time blindness*: described, not named—"blind to time, or nearsighted toward the future" (p.5).

Other claims: hyperactivity usually declines by adulthood, leaving restlessness (p.7); DSM-5 adult parenthetical clarifications "should likely be ignored until more research is done" (p.11); untreated ADHD linked to higher mortality by midlife (p.7).

**Step One opener (pp.3–4).** Quotes from adults only (e.g., "Time escapes me…"; "I have to do things as soon as I think of them, because if I don't, I'll forget about them" p.3). No method.
