# Chapter 21. Rule 6: Break It Down … and Make It Matter
Pages 183–188. (Core chapter for planning, chunking, planners.)

1. **Main idea.** Rule 6 "brings the future a lot closer" (p.183). When pictures, words and feelings fail, use external motivators: break goals into very small work quotas, make yourself accountable to someone, and reward each small step (pp.184–188). "Virtually everyone with ADHD benefits from following Rule 6" (p.183).
2. **Named method/routine.** Rule 6, three strategies. **(A) Break down longer-term tasks into much smaller units** (pp.184–186): same-day project → 1-hour or, better, half-hour chunks; write what you'll do in each period and run a highlighter over the current step (p.185). End-of-week project → decide the amount per day (e.g., one closet per day), sequence dependent tasks, get help from a boss/mentor, past reports, office files or colleagues; then break each day into 1-hour or half-hour segments (p.185). For work interleaved with other projects → pick a time block, write it in the day planner or desk calendar, then split it into smaller segments (p.185). Michael Phelps's schedule in 15-minute units as example; "mark your day planner beforehand with how you plan to spend each hour," then break each hour into 15-minute microtask units (p.185). Breaks between chunks are "just as important" (p.186); "five at a time" (not 30); athletes do 10 reps at a time (p.186). Sign: "CHUNK IT, BABY!" (p.186). **(B) Make yourself accountable to someone else** (pp.186–187): tell them what you plan (usually the chunks) and notify them as each smaller objective is done; supportive coworker/supervisor/mentor at work, partner/spouse/roommate/neighbor at home; reward the helper (lunch every couple weeks, token gift, gift certificate, favorite coffee/tea, p.187). **(C) Give yourself little rewards as you accomplish each small objective** (p.187): e.g., cappuccino after 5 pages in 30–60 minutes; bass guitar once; Hershey's Kiss; a minute of the view (p.187). "Putting it all together" after each chunk, do four things: congratulate yourself; short break (a few minutes); tell someone if available; small, brief reward (p.188). Summary: chunk it; make goals public; take brief breaks; reward after each (p.188).
3. **Tools/containers, one job each.** Day planner or desk calendar: hold the written time blocks and microtasks (p.185). Highlighter: mark the step you're working on "to keep your attention focused on that and off the rest" (p.185). Sign "CHUNK IT, BABY!": cue on desk (p.186). Accountability partner: knows whether you did it (p.186). Rewards: small incentive per quota (p.187). Office files / past reports: model for how to break down the project (p.185).
4. **Notes/thoughts/reference.** Reference: past reports and office files of similar work to model the breakdown (p.185). Notes/ideas capture: not addressed.
5. **Transfers/review.** Plan the day's chunks "beforehand" in the planner (p.185). After each chunk: congratulate, break, report, reward (p.188). The accountability partner receives notice of each subgoal and final goal (p.186). No weekly or end-of-day review is specified.
6. **Worksheets/deadlines.** Prompts: Do you panic at distant deadlines? Do complex projects overwhelm you? Trouble working without supervision? (p.184). Fill-in: "What small rewards could you easily give yourself? At work / At home / Elsewhere" (p.187). Deadline rule: "The further the deadline, the more essential it is to break it down and the smaller you need to make the chunks" (p.185).
7. **Key terms.** *Work quota / chunk*: a small unit of work (p.184–185). *Microtask*: a task within a 15-minute unit (p.185). *Accountability*: "We are far, far more likely to follow through on the goals we set for ourselves if we tell someone else" (p.186). *Artificially motivate yourself* (p.188).
