# gaps.md — things the book points to but never explains, or contradicts itself on
Book: Barkley, *Taking Charge of Adult ADHD*, 2nd ed. (2022). Page numbers are the print-page markers embedded in the EPUB. Items marked "unverified" could not be checked against the text I read.

## A. Organizing / capture / review (most relevant to your journal-ecosystem work)
1. **No integration rule for journal, to-do list, planner, and calendar.** Ch.19 prescribes a pocket journal (record of commitments, pp.175–177) and a to-do list (pp.174–175); ch.10 mentions a day planner and to-do list (p.98); ch.21 uses a day planner or desk calendar (p.185); ch.24 adds a daily assignment calendar, a journal, a notebook system, and colored folders (p.204). The book never says how an entry moves from one to another, which one is the master, or when. Only the journal-check cadence is given (ch.19 p.177). Ch.10 p.98 does describe one move: task captured in notebook/phone → broken into smaller steps in a day planner or to-do list.
2. **No review cadence for notes.** Journal: "refer to it often, hourly if need be," with a 15–30 minute alarm (ch.19 p.177); "several times a day" (ch.24 p.204). No daily or weekly review of the whole system, no clean-up, no archiving, no purge of finished items. Not addressed anywhere.
3. **"Hourly if need be" vs. "alarm every 15 to 30 minutes"** for checking the journal (ch.19 p.177) are not reconciled.
4. **Journal contents are not structured.** Format, layout, one running log vs. sections, and how long to keep it are not specified (ch.19 pp.175–177). The same journal appears to hold both commitments (p.176) and task-duration data (p.175); the text says "a journal" for both and does not say whether it is one object (unverified).
5. **Journal medium.** Paper or smartphone are both allowed (ch.10 p.98; ch.19 p.176), yet ch.19 p.176 says people use a paper pocket journal more reliably than a digital recorder. A phone notes app is not discussed directly.
6. **Reference material and non-task thoughts are never addressed.** The book's tools hold tasks, promises, deadlines, rules, and cues. Ideas, reference material, and long-term notes: not addressed (checked ch.10, 19, 21, 22, 24, 25, 26). Closest items: SQ4R reading notes (ch.24 pp.206–207), meeting notes (ch.25 p.222), receipt file (ch.26 p.230).
7. **No master tool list.** Ch.19 p.172 says tools are "listed in Chapter 10" (pp.98–100). Other chapters add different tools (ch.22 p.192 sticky notes/magnets/cards; ch.24 pp.204–205 colored folders, MotivAider, Smartpen; ch.25 p.221 e-mail calendar). The book never consolidates them.
8. **Rule numbering slip.** Ch.22 p.193 calls Rule 7 the "advanced" version of Rule 2 and says Rule 2 advised writing things down or posting a sign; that is Rule 4 (ch.19). Rule 2 (ch.17) is visualizing the past.
9. **Chunk size.** Ch.21 gives 1-hour, half-hour, and 15-minute units (p.185), "five at a time" (p.186) and "a few minutes" breaks (p.188) without saying how to choose.
10. **Symptom Tracking Scale.** Ch.15 p.150 suggests using it to monitor medication but gives no frequency or start rule; it is framed for sharing with a doctor (p.153).
11. **Accountability meeting load.** Coach twice a day for 5 minutes (ch.24 p.203; ch.25 p.220), supervisor check-ins "several times a day" (ch.10 p.98), and supervisor reviews every 3–6 weeks (ch.25 p.223) are separate cadences; no combined schedule is given.

## B. Internal contradictions or inconsistent figures
12. **Therapy stance.** Ch.10 p.100: "Avoid talk- or insight-oriented therapy, psychoanalysis, weekly group therapy focusing on complaining." Ch.12 p.118 endorses CBT for executive deficits, coaching, and support groups; ch.15 p.152 says therapists and life coaches "can be of huge assistance"; ch.11 p.102 recommends counseling for grief. The distinction (point-of-performance vs. other therapy) is implied but not stated.
13. **Executive-function count.** Ch.6 p.48 lists six (working memory as one); ch.9 p.70 says "a total of seven" (splits working memory in two) but p.88 says the author "combine[s]" self-motivation with emotional self-control. Unreconciled.
14. **Symptom-count labels.** Ch.1 p.6: "91 additional symptoms"; ch.5 p.28: nine of "109 symptoms"; ch.5 p.31: "91 symptoms." Ch.6 p.46 gives 18 DSM + 91 more, so 109 = 18 + 91 (inferred; never stated).
15. **Diagnostic threshold.** Ch.1 p.10: ≥4 of the first 7 or 6 of 9; ch.1 p.13: "at least four to six of the nine"; ch.5 pp.27–28: "just four symptoms is enough"; DSM needs five for adults (ch.5 p.27; Appendix p.269).
16. **Onset age.** Ch.1 pp.8, 13: before 16; ch.5 p.26: before 12–16.
17. **Medication response rates.** Ch.2 p.17 and ch.12 p.116: fewer than 10% have no response to any drug; ch.12 p.113 (Step Three opener): "70–95%" improved; ch.14 p.138: ~75%; ch.15 p.145: "up to 80%"; p.146: 75% first drug, 80–90% overall; p.148: 10–25% do not respond to the first drug.
18. **Medication groupings.** Ch.12 p.120: nonstimulants = atomoxetine and viloxazine, alpha-2 = guanfacine XR and clonidine XR. Ch.14 p.137: "the nonstimulants" = atomoxetine or guanfacine XR.
19. **Approval status.** Ch.12 p.121 table is introduced as "FDA approved for adult ADHD" and lists viloxazine and clonidine XR, with footnotes not marking them adult-approved; ch.14 pp.138, 143 say both may be approved for adults "soon." Drug-status claims may be dated (unverified).
20. **Offspring risk.** Ch.6 p.46: 40–57% (and "up to eight times"); ch.27 p.237: 30–54%; ch.27 p.238: 20–50%.
21. **Mortality.** Ch.28 p.244: children twice as likely to die before 10; adults over four times more likely by 45; p.247: 2.5 times more likely to die prematurely; p.249: children three times more likely to die before 46. No sources given in the text; life-expectancy loss of 11–13 years (p.244) is "my own recent research," uncited (unverified).
22. **Planning horizon.** Ch.9 p.72: 36-year-old plans 6–12 weeks ahead; p.78: ages 20–30 plan 8–12 weeks.
23. **Self-report reliability age.** Ch.1 p.9: 27–32; ch.9 pp.75–76: "Until age 30 or older."
24. **Stimulant duration.** Ch.13 p.123: 8–12 hours (vs 3–5); ch.13 p.135 (Vyvanse) 10–14 hours; ch.12 table p.122 (Vyvanse) "at least 8 hours"; Concerta 12 hours in table vs "8–12 hours or more" (ch.13 p.133).
25. **Co-occurring disorders.** Ch.2 p.16 order (anxiety first) vs ch.29 p.255 (ODD first, "roughly most to least prevalent"; p.256 says order is "not set in stone"). Bipolar: ch.2 p.16 lists it as co-occurring more often; ch.29 p.261 says ADHD adults are generally not at higher risk, only the reverse.

## C. Pointers to material that is missing or misdirected
26. **Wrong chapter reference.** Ch.29 p.255 attributes the "80% or more have another disorder" statement to Chapter 1; it appears in ch.2 (p.16).
27. **Ch.29 promised coverage.** Lists personality disorders and eating disorders (p.255) but gives no section for them; the chapter ends after bipolar disorder (p.261). Substance use is deferred to ch.30.
28. **Brain-imaging "box" on pp.103–105** (ch.6 p.46): the matching text appears as a bulleted research summary in ch.11 (pp.103–104). Whether it is a visual box in print is unverified from the EPUB. The NIMH study on p.111 (ch.7 p.60) is confirmed (ch.11 p.111).
29. **Download box.** Ch.15 p.154 refers to a box "at the end of the Contents" for photocopy/download of scales; I did not locate it (unverified).
30. **"My other book on executive functions"** (ch.9 p.70) is not named in that chapter; the Resources list it as *Executive Functions* (2012), p.278.
31. **Possible blurb mismatch in Resources.** On the Nadeau college-guide entry the blurb says "Although intended for professionals … latest research findings on … psychological treatments" (p.279–280), and the Ramsay & Rostain CBT entry blurb is about "women with ADHD" (p.280). These look swapped or shifted (unverified; could be an EPUB conversion issue).
32. **Condition-specific advice with no research base**, stated by the author: dating/marriage (ch.27 p.235), sex and contraception (ch.28 p.250), parenting approaches (ch.27 pp.238–240). Treat as the author's commonsense suggestions.
