# Chapter 27. Relationships
Pages 232–241.

1. **Main idea.** Three aspects of self-control wreck relationships: emotions taking over, loss of self-awareness, and missing the "scripts" for entering, shifting and ending interactions (pp.233–234). Barkley notes no studies exist yet on any specific approach to ADHD and dating/marriage, so his advice is "commonsense ideas flowing from what research we do have" (p.235).
2. **Named method/routine.** No named system. Relationship advice (pp.235–236): get a diagnosis/treatment; read Gina Pera's *Is It You, Me, or Adult A.D.D.?*; consider mindfulness meditation class (Kabat-Zinn-style practices; Zylowska book); apply Step Four; be your "best self" (think first, track what hurts/helps your partner, remember dates with external cues, listen, show tact, do a fair share of chores). Parenting (pp.238–240): evaluate/treat yourself and your child; schedule special time with external cues; **set an external timer every 15–30 minutes while supervising children** (pp.238–239); talk through family rules with the other parent, write them down and post them (p.239); take a behavioral parent-training class (after treating your own ADHD); time-out for yourself; weekly respite (p.240). Dual-parenting delegation list (p.240).
3. **Tools/containers, one job each.** External cues for date reminders (birthdays, anniversaries), same as doctor's appointments and deadlines (p.236). External timer set to 15–30 minute intervals for child monitoring (p.239). Written, posted family rules (p.239). External cues for scheduled child time and rewards (p.238).
4. **Notes/thoughts/reference.** Written family rules posted at home (p.239). Dates → external cues (p.236). Reference books: Pera; Orlov (two books); Tuckman (sex and ADHD); Zylowska; Solanto CBT; Nay *Taking Charge of Anger*; Barkley et al. *Your Defiant Child/Teen* (pp.234, 236, 239). Where to file notes: not addressed.
5. **Transfers/review.** Not addressed.
6. **Worksheets/deadlines.** None. Statistics: dating relationships 4–5 times more likely to be fair/poor; marriage quality more than twofold lower (p.235); 30–54% of children of adults with ADHD have ADHD (p.237) and chance elsewhere stated as 20–50% (p.238) (see gaps.md).
7. **Key terms.** *Life's scripts*: knowing when to start, shift, end or keep an interaction going; "reading entrance and exit cues is just as important as what you do onstage" (p.234). *Self-monitoring in encounters*: read bodily and facial cues, perceive emotional nuances in voice, adjust (p.234). *Smart dual parenting*: delegate homework, time-sensitive events, major discipline pre-approval, and driving to the parent without ADHD (p.240).
