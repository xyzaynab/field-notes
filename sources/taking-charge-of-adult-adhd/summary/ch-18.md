# Chapter 18. Rule 3: Say the Past … and Then the Future
Pages 166–170.

1. **Main idea.** Rule 3 helps you "analyze the situation before deciding what to do" and "develop rules you can use for the same situations in the future" (p.166). Use words (the mind's voice) to describe the mental images from Rule 2 and extract rules; strengthening one of the mind's eye or voice can compensate for the other (p.167).
2. **Named method/routine.** Rule 3. Steps: (a) pick a modest goal you've failed at because of weak verbal working memory—e.g., a weekly small deposit into savings before managing investments (p.167); (b) "Become your own interviewer": discuss the images with yourself, label them, describe them, extract a rule for next time (p.168); imagine holding a microphone; ask the question set—"What's going on here?"; "Have I been in a situation like this before?"; "What's the same and what's different?"; "What did I do last time?"; "Is that an option here?"; "Are there better choices?"; "If I decide to do X, what will happen?"; "Have I seen X [a successful person you admire] do this?"; "If my decision turns out badly, how will I feel tomorrow?" (pp.168–169); (c) "Narrate what's happening out loud" (whispered is fine; hands-free-phone pretense in public) (p.169); (d) give yourself instructions while at a task: keep at it, be careful, complete each step, finish (pp.169–170). Practice settings: working at home deciding whether to keep at it, driving alone, jogging/walking (p.169). Time: not stated.
3. **Tools/containers.** None physical. The mind's voice is the tool: job is to turn images into rules and instructions (p.168). Imagined microphone (job: structure self-interview, p.168).
4. **Notes/thoughts/reference.** Rules are extracted verbally; writing them down is not prescribed in this chapter (it is in Rule 4). Example rules: wait until after an open meeting to express an opinion that might not be well received; know who your allies are among coworkers (pp.166–167).
5. **Transfers/review.** Not addressed.
6. **Worksheets/deadlines.** Diagnostic questions (reading others' emotions; following rules; retaining reading; dropping diets/plans) (p.169). No deadline.
7. **Key terms.** *Mind's voice*: verbal working memory (p.167). Weak verbal working memory and misdemeanors: "not malevolence … but a fundamental failure to apply or follow the rules at key moments" (p.167). "Externally vocalized words, even when just whispered, are more powerful at controlling and guiding our behavior than words we may say in our mind" (p.169).
