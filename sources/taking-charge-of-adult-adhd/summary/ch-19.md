# Chapter 19. Rule 4: Externalize Key Information
Pages 171–177. (Core chapter for tools and capture.)

1. **Main idea.** Rule 4 gives "something to rely on besides your own memory" (p.171). Build external cues and records because "something external, in front of us, is a far more potent reminder than an image or word held in our mind" (p.173). Adults get no star charts or picture charts, so "it's up to you to create and use external reminders" (pp.171–172).
2. **Named method/routine.** Rule 4, three strategies. (A) **Put physical cues in plain view in problem situations**—must be "in plain sight, right in the place where you need the reminder" (p.173). (B) **Make a list of steps or procedures** to follow next time you meet a problem task, including to-do lists (pp.174–175). (C) **Carry a journal with you all the time** (pp.175–177). First, find where mental effort still fails: staying silent; gathering tools/materials; carried-over mental to-do items; emotions (anger, frustration, guilt, shame) (p.172). Time: carry journal all day; refer to it "often, hourly if need be"; set smartphone alarm "every 15 to 30 minutes" to check it (p.177).
3. **Tools/containers, one job each.**
- **Signs/pictures at the work spot** (monitor margin or desk sign: don't surf the web; boss's picture "Get to Work!"; "Do it for her!" photo): block a known impulse in place (pp.173–174).
- **Note in the wallet pocket / sticky note on credit card**: ask whether you need to make the purchase (p.174).
- **Card on smartphone screen**: ask whether the call can wait (p.174).
- **Prize photo (e.g., tennis trophy)**: sustain practice (p.174). **Stop-sign symbol**: stop before deciding (p.174). **List of rules for a recurring complicated report** (every 2 weeks): follow the procedure (p.174). **Photo/stop sign on the refrigerator**: stop impulsive eating (p.174).
- **To-do list** placed "where you can't miss it": remind you what to do now or next when you drift (pp.174–175). Reviewed by a sympathetic spouse/mentor/sibling/roommate if you over-write it (p.175).
- **Journal (small pocket notebook/bound journal, or smartphone)**: (1) "your record of what you need to do"—dates, commitments, promises, appointments, deadlines, instructions anyone gives you (p.175–176); (2) a log of how long tasks take so you can budget time (p.175). "It's your external working memory." (p.177) Paper pocket journal recommended over digital recorder because people forget to replay recordings (p.176).
- **Smartphone alarm**: prompt to check the journal every 15–30 minutes (p.177).
- Chapter 10 holds the broader tool list (p.172).
4. **Notes/thoughts/reference.** Instructions, promises, deadlines, dates, appointments → the journal, written the moment you hear them: "Write in it anything someone says to you that you are to do, any promise you make, any commitment to others, date, deadline, meeting" (p.176). Task duration data → journal, used later to budget time (p.175). Rules/procedures for recurring tasks → a written list or posted cue (p.174). Reference material: not addressed. Placement: journal in an outer purse flap or pocket "where you can see and reach it easily" (p.176).
5. **Transfers/review.** The journal is read "often, hourly if need be" with a 15–30 minute alarm (p.177). A to-do list can be checked by another person (p.175). No stated step for moving journal entries into the to-do list, calendar, or filing, and no daily/weekly review or cleanup of the journal (see gaps.md). Chapter 10 separately says to translate journal tasks into smaller steps in a day planner (ch.10 p.98).
6. **Worksheets/deadlines.** Self-diagnostic prompts under "Where do you still have a lot of trouble…" (pp.172–173). Instruction with a deadline-like start: "Today, before you have a chance to forget, start using your journal" (p.176). "Don't consider yourself completely dressed in the morning until you slip that journal and pencil or other note-taking device into your pocket" (p.176).
7. **Key terms.** *Externalize*: put key information into physical form where the problem occurs (ch.10 p.98; ch.19 p.173). *Physical cues*: "your stand-in for a shaky working memory" (p.173). *External working memory*: the journal (p.177). Reliability stakes: being seen as forgetful and untrustworthy causes "more shame and distress than just about any other shortcoming" (p.176).
Q&A (p.175): a reader who wrote a three-page to-do list for one day; advice is to have someone review it and to log task times in the journal.
