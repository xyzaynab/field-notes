# Chapter 12. Why It Makes Sense to Try Medication
Pages 115–122. (Step Three)

1. **Main idea.** "Medication works": ADHD medications are "the most effective treatments currently available" (p.115), normalizing behavior in 50–65% and substantially improving another 20–30% (p.115). They correct or compensate for the underlying neurology only while in the bloodstream and brain, so benefits last only while you keep taking them (p.116). Author discloses paid consulting/speaking for several pharmaceutical companies (footnote, p.122).
2. **Named method/routine.** None. Decision points: three FDA-approved groups for adults—stimulants (methylphenidate, amphetamine), nonstimulants (atomoxetine, viloxazine), alpha-2 drugs (guanfacine XR, clonidine XR) (p.120). Barkley's position, 5 points (p.121): needs thorough evaluation first; myths abound; nothing is "etched in stone" (you can switch or stop); prescribe collaboratively; ADHD is neurogenetic so medication is reasonable. Reference table of drugs, durations, forms (pp.121–122). Time: durations in table, e.g., Concerta 12 hours; Ritalin 3–4 hours (pp.121–122).
3. **Tools/containers.** None. Medication table is the "handy reference you might end up coming back to again and again" (p.121).
4. **Notes/thoughts/reference.** Not addressed beyond: resources starting p.278 for updates on how medications work (p.118).
5. **Transfers/review.** Not addressed.
6. **Worksheets/deadlines.** Self-questions about doubts (dependence, "weakness", p.120). Key question: "Do I deserve to have a tougher life than other people just because I was born with a biological predisposition to ADHD?" (pp.119–120). No deadline.
7. **Key terms.**
- *Subclinical ADHD / ADHD NOS*: some symptoms and impairment but too few for DSM criteria (p.118). Barkley: "ask for the medicine even if you are a symptom or two shy" (p.118).
- *Mechanism*: dopamine and norepinephrine not available "in the same measure"; medication increases communication between nerve cells (p.117).
- Brain regions: prefrontal, striatal, anterior cingulate, cerebellum; 2–3 years behind in children/teens (p.116).
- Diabetic/insulin analogy (p.118). Not "Band-Aids" (p.118).
Findings: at least half of diagnosed children still meet criteria as adults; 85% of those diagnosed as adults had other disorders, especially anxiety/depression (p.119). Medication alone is not the only treatment: CBT for executive deficits, coaching, support groups (p.118).
