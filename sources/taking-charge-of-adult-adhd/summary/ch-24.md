# Chapter 24. Education
Pages 199–209. (Step Five, "Change Your Situation"; opener pp.197–198.)

**Step Five opener (pp.197–198).** A bar chart (from *ADHD in Adults: What the Science Says*, p.198) shows adults with ADHD impaired in every life area. Read first the chapters for your hardest domains; ideas "flow from the rules in Step Four" (p.197).

1. **Main idea.** Education is probably the hardest domain for people with ADHD (p.199). Plan for it: know your history and any learning disabilities, get accommodations under the ADA, apply the eight rules ("your bible," p.203), reconsider medication, and use specific tools and study tactics (pp.200–209).
2. **Named method/routine.**
- **Coach/mentor routine** (p.203): meet "twice a day for 5 minutes"—morning meeting to review your to-do list or day's goals; late-day meeting to show what you accomplished.
- **SQ4R reading method** (p.206–207): Survey (leaf through); draft Questions; then for each paragraph Read, Recite aloud softly, Write in notebook, Review what you wrote; later extend to two paragraphs or a page.
- **"Time off the clock"** for timed tests (p.207): use a stopwatch; stop it for 1–2 minute breaks as often as you like; total test time equals what others get; "self-pacing."
- Other: schedule faculty/adviser meetings every 3–6 weeks (p.207); exercise before exams or boring classes (3–5 minutes may help, p.206); regular exercise 3–4 times a week for 20–30 minutes (p.209).
3. **Tools/containers, one job each (pp.203–207).**
- **Daily assignment calendar**: set goals for each day, track appointments; used to review the day's tasks with your coach; kept in a visible spot (p.204).
- **Journal**: write "everything you need to do, including assignments, individual requests made of you by teachers, meetings you've scheduled, promises you've made or that others have made to you—anything of the slightest importance"; "Review this journal several times a day" (p.204; see ch.19 for tips).
- **Notebook organizing system, day planners, optional personal time-management/organizing app**: stay organized; caution that phones can distract (p.204).
- **Separate colored folders for each class**: hold completed assignments so they aren't misplaced (p.204).
- **Tactile cueing device (MotivAider, vibrates at set or random intervals)**: frequent prompts to stay on task (pp.204–205).
- **Tape/digital recorder or Smartpen (livescribe.com)**: capture lectures (p.205).
- **Computer for papers**: avoid slow, illegible handwriting (p.206).
- Liaison and accommodations: college ADHD liaison in special student services reviews documentation, describes accommodations, works with professors, links to counseling/psychiatry (p.204).
- Medication: long-acting forms cover 8–14 hours; possible immediate-release dose late in the day (p.203).
4. **Notes/thoughts/reference.** Assignments, requests, meetings, promises → the journal (p.204). Completed assignments → a colored folder per class (p.204). Lectures → recorded, plus continuous note-taking "keeping your hand moving, even if you don't really need to write down the information" (pp.205–206). Reading notes → written paragraph by paragraph in a notebook via SQ4R (p.206). Extra curriculum materials from teachers/library (videos, notes, articles) (p.205). Ideas not tied to a class: not addressed.
5. **Transfers/review.** Journal reviewed several times a day (p.204). Daily assignment calendar reviewed with coach in the morning and late afternoon (pp.203–204). Faculty/adviser reviews every 3–6 weeks (p.207). How journal items move to the daily calendar: not stated.
6. **Worksheets/deadlines.** Tables of school outcomes for adults diagnosed as adults vs as children (p.201–202), e.g., college graduation 30% vs 9%; special education 35% vs 65%; diagnosed learning disorder 28% vs 45% (p.202). LD defined as scoring below the 14th percentile on standardized tests (p.201). Assignment deadlines: bring documentation to the college before seeking accommodations (p.202). Reasoning to reconsider medication "now" if starting school or a challenging job (p.203).
7. **Key terms.** *Specific learning disability (LD)*: "a delay in a specific area of basic academic skills, such as reading, spelling, math, and reading comprehension" (p.201). *Reasonable accommodations*: under the ADA (p.202); "this law exists to help you help yourself" (p.203). *Coach or mentor*: "someone to whom you can make yourself accountable every day" (p.203). *Time off the clock* (p.207). *Peer tutoring*: alternate instructor/student roles (p.207). *Fall-back classmate* (p.207).
Also: avoid caffeine and nicotine as self-medication (p.209).
