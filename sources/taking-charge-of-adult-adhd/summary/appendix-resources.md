# Back matter: Appendix and Resources (not chapters; summarized for completeness)
Pages 269–284.

**Appendix, "A Closer Look at ADHD Symptoms" (pp.269–276).** Reproduces the DSM-5 criteria for ADHD: 18 symptoms, 9 inattention and 9 hyperactivity–impulsivity; for adults 17 and older at least five required; symptoms persisting ≥6 months (p.269). Notes the criteria were developed for children (p.269). Then "Additional Symptoms Associated with ADHD in Adults" (pp.272–276): a table of 91 symptoms with percent of adults with ADHD vs community (e.g., "Find it difficult to tolerate waiting; impatient," 75% vs 5%, p.272). I did not summarize the 91 rows.

**Resources (pp.277–284).** Barkley states the book relies on two NIMH-funded studies: the "Milwaukee study" (children followed into adulthood) and the "UMASS study" (adult clinic referrals vs clinical and community comparison groups) (p.277). Sections: Sources of Scientific Data (p.277); Additional Reading (pp.278–281); organizations, websites, coaching directories and professional-finding advice (pp.281–284). Organizing/time-management titles listed with the author's one-line blurbs: Kolberg & Nadeau, *ADD-Friendly Ways to Organize Your Life* ("excellent suggestions for improving the organizational and other executive function deficits," p.279); Levrini & Prevatt, *Succeeding with Adult ADHD* (p.279); Ramsay & Rostain, *The Adult ADHD Tool Kit* (p.280); Surman & Bilkey, *Fast Minds* ("a fine introduction to adult ADHD with clear, effective solutions for managing it," p.281); Wilder, *Help for Women with ADHD* ("easy-to-follow recommendations for dealing with time management and organization," p.281). Coaching directories: ADD Coach Academy, ADHD Coaches Organization, International Coaching Federation (p.284). Barkley notes much of the additional reading "is based on clinical experience instead of research studies" (p.278). *Unapologetically ADHD* did not appear in my search of the book text.
