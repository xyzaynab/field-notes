# Chapter 22. Rule 7: Make Problems External, Physical, and Manual
Pages 189–193.

1. **Main idea.** Rule 7 "simplifies problem solving" (p.189). Because working memory is weak, make the problem tangible—visual, manipulable, outside your head—so you don't rely on working memory and your emotions "don't erupt with the frustration of trying to do it all in your head" (p.189).
2. **Named method/routine.** Rule 7: "Use physical, external tools to solve problems whenever you can" (p.192). Steps: (a) know where your most complicated/urgent problems arise—work, home, social, practical (repairs, shopping, vendors) (p.190); (b) know what kind of learner you are—visual, auditory, tactile, kinesthetic (p.191); (c) get all the data pertaining to the problem "in pieces—on paper, using objects, or graphically represented" (pp.192–193); (d) move the pieces with hands, eyes, ears, whole body to see whether a solution appears (p.193). Time: not stated.
3. **Tools/containers, one job each.** Computer lists; sticky notes or magnets on a board; audiotapes; graphic devices (p.192): get data out of your head so you can see/feel/hear/manipulate it. Paper, 3˝ × 5˝ file cards, laptop: for verbal problems (p.193). Scratch-paper doodles/sketches (p.193). Mock-ups (moving boxes as a kitchen), form boards, simulation software (p.193).
4. **Notes/thoughts/reference.** Problem data: break into pieces on cards/paper/sticky notes and arrange them physically (pp.192–193). Where finished work is kept: not addressed.
5. **Transfers/review.** Not addressed.
6. **Worksheets/deadlines.** Assessment prompts about problem-solving at work/home/social/practical (p.190); learner-type prompts (p.191). No deadline.
7. **Key terms.** *External, physical, manual*: the three qualities of Rule 7 (title). Call it "the 'advanced' version of Rule 2" (p.193)—but see gaps.md. "You need to be able to use your senses to oil the gears of your mind" (p.189).
