# Chapter 3. Where Can You Go to Get Help?
Pages 19–21.

1. **Main idea.** Find a professional experienced with adult ADHD, starting with your primary care physician if you trust them; use referral sources and screening questions before booking (ch.3 pp.19–21).
2. **Named method/routine.** None. A referral-source list (PCP; state psychiatric/psychological association; CHADD; ADDA; university medical school psychiatry; hospital psychiatry; university psychology clinic; county mental health center; online search incl. chadd.org and additudemag.com; trusted friend/relative's practitioner) (pp.19–20). Time: not stated.
3. **Tools/containers.** None for personal organization.
4. **Notes/thoughts/reference.** Not addressed.
5. **Transfers/review.** Not addressed.
6. **Worksheets/deadlines.** A 9-item "Questions to Ask before You Make an Appointment" call script (p.21): share of practice with ADHD; adult share; years treating adults; specialty (psychiatry, clinical psychology, neuropsychology, behavioral neurology); board certification; wait time; whether they treat after diagnosing; nearby resources (coaching, skills training, support groups); fees/insurance. No deadline.
7. **Key terms.** None defined.
