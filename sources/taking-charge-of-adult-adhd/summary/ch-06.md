# Chapter 6. Know Your ADHD
Pages 45–57. (Step Two opener)

1. **Main idea.** ADHD is neurological and highly heritable (p.46). Its ~109 symptoms (18 DSM + 91 more, p.46) cluster into poor inhibition, poor self-regulation, and executive-function problems (pp.46–47), seen in five problem areas (pp.48–55). Adult ADHD is "a problem with the ability to organize behavior over time to prepare for the future"—"a form of time blindness" (p.56). The problem is performance, not knowledge: "not using what you know at critical points of performance" (p.57).
2. **Named method/routine.** Reading routine: read a chapter or several daily, "even skimming at first"; alarm on computer/phone/watch to "read for 15 minutes at a certain time every day" (p.45). Five-area self-check (check off problems you've experienced, pp.48–55). 
3. **Tools/containers.** Computer, phone or wristwatch alarm: one job—cue a daily 15-minute reading session (p.45). Resources list at back of book for further sources (p.45). No other tools.
4. **Notes/thoughts/reference.** Not addressed beyond: be skeptical and read widely; ch.11 end gives guidance for judging information (p.45).
5. **Transfers/review.** Not addressed.
6. **Worksheets/deadlines.** The five problem-area checklists: (1) time, planning, goals (p.49); (2) self-organization, problem solving, working memory (pp.50–51); (3) self-restraint/inhibition (p.52); (4) self-motivation (pp.53–54); (5) emotional self-regulation (pp.54–55). Prevalence table, ADHD vs community, self-report and others' reports (pp.55–56): time management 98% vs 8%; emotional control 98% vs 7% (self-report). No deadline.
7. **Key terms.**
- *Self-control*: "any response or set of responses that you direct at yourself and your own likely behavior that would lead you to do something different from what your first impulse would dictate" (p.48).
- *Inhibition*: "your psychological braking system" (p.48).
- *Executive functions*: "the specific self-directed actions that we use to control ourselves … mental abilities … to consider our past and then anticipate the future"; "like a GPS in your brain" (p.48). Listed: inhibition, self-awareness, working memory, emotional self-regulation, self-motivation, planning, problem solving (p.48). Using them "takes will and effort" (p.48).
- *Time blindness*: "ADHD is a form of time blindness" (p.56).
- *Heritability figures*: 40–57% of children of a parent with ADHD have it; eight times more likely (p.46).

**Step Two opener (pp.41–43).** A diagnosis is "like holding a passport to a better life" (p.41): medication, psychological therapies, coaching, strengths strategies, compensating tools, coping skills, peer support, ADA accommodations (pp.41–42). "Owning your ADHD" = "accepting ADHD as part of your psychological makeup" (p.42). Process: "You start by coming to know it, then you own it, and finally you're able to work with it" (p.43).
