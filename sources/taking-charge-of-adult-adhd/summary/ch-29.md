# Chapter 29. Other Mental and Emotional Problems
Pages 255–261.

1. **Main idea.** If medication and the book's strategies aren't enough, consider another condition: "Only 1 in 5 adults with ADHD has ADHD alone" (p.255). A professional evaluation is the only way to confirm any of them (p.256). Call the doctor who diagnosed you and ask for a consultation, describing leftover impairments (p.256).
2. **Named method/routine.** Screening question sets (yes-count clues) for each condition: ODD (anger, rule breaking, defensiveness, firings, pp.256–257); CD/ASPD (trouble with law, runaway, truancy, record, bar fights, harassment, pp.258); anxiety (nervousness, worry, phobias, social fear, public speaking, p.259); depression (hard to get out of bed, blue most days, irritability, appetite change, loss of interest, p.260). Treatments per condition (author's summaries): ODD—ADHD medication may help; anger management course; Nay's *Taking Charge of Anger*; sometimes a second medication (pp.257–258). CD/ASPD—as for ODD, plus substance-use help; leave antisocial peer scenes, hobbies, better social network, move if necessary (p.259). Anxiety—CBT, medications; atomoxetine "does not exacerbate anxiety" (p.260). Depression—antidepressants and CBT (p.261). Time: not stated.
3. **Tools/containers.** None for organization. Resources: anger-management course at clinic/community college; W. Robert Nay book (p.258).
4. **Notes/thoughts/reference.** Not addressed, except to prepare a description of symptoms and leftover impairments for the doctor (p.256).
5. **Transfers/review.** Not addressed.
6. **Worksheets/deadlines.** Four yes/no question lists (above). No deadline. Reported rates: 80%+ have at least one other disorder, more than 50% at least two (p.255); ODD persists into adulthood in half of kids who have it (p.257); 17–35% of adults with ADHD recall CD-level behavior; 7–21% of those still with ADHD meet ASPD (pp.258–259); anxiety 17–52% of adults (p.260); depression risk more than three times general population; fell from 28% (childhood) to 18% (age 27) in Wisconsin study (p.261); bipolar: risk of ADHD 20–25% adult-onset, 35–45% adolescent-onset, 80–97% childhood-onset (p.261).
7. **Key terms.** *ODD*: feeling "like a misfit, always the 'bad boy'" (p.257). *CD*: "not just defiant … lie, steal, fight, carry weapons, set fires, and/or commit sexual assault" (p.258). *ASPD*: "essentially CD grown up" (p.259). *Anxiety*: "abnormal fears and worries, mainly unrealistic ones" (p.260), though for ADHD adults avoiding past humiliation may seem reasonable (p.260).
