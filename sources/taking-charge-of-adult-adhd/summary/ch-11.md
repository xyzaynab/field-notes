# Chapter 11. Own Your ADHD
Pages 101–111. (Ends Step Two.)

1. **Main idea.** Accept the diagnosis as "an explanation, not an excuse" (p.102): ADHD "is not your fault … but accepting it is your responsibility" (p.103). Since there is no cure, change your environment—build "ramps" (p.102)—and become a skeptical consumer of ADHD information (pp.106–111).
2. **Named method/routine.** Environment-shaping options (p.105–106): choose environments where you can succeed (e.g., sales, performing arts, entrepreneur, travel/meetings roles, administrative assistant, mentor); surround yourself with supportive people; use the best ADHD resources. "Consumer's guide" source checks (pp.108–111): check the source (profit? agenda? size/history? research center?), check the content (too good to be true? hard facts? references? how much evidence?). Time: not stated.
3. **Tools/containers.** No organizing tools. Information tools: Google Scholar (browse journals; set up weekly email of new articles, p.107); university "grand rounds" lectures and YouTube (p.107); organizations list (CHADD, ADDA, ADD Resources, ADD WareHouse, World Federation of ADHD, ADDConsults, LDA, ADDISS, CADDRA, CADDAC, p.108); videos "Failing at Normal" and Jessica McCabe/Rick Green (p.102, URLs as given in book).
4. **Notes/thoughts/reference.** Reference material: read widely, spot-check references are real (p.110–111), use Resources at the back of the book (p.107). How to store or file what you read: not addressed.
5. **Transfers/review.** Not addressed. One scheduling element: Google Scholar weekly email (p.107).
6. **Worksheets/deadlines.** A reaction checklist (denial, relief, demoralized, angry, sad/angry, p.101). None other. Barkley suggests counseling if grief persists (p.102).
7. **Key terms.**
- *Ramps*: "the ADHD equivalent of wheelchair ramps"; they "do not correct a disorder, but … reduce or eliminate the impairments" (p.102).
- *Accountability*: make consequences "more frequent, immediate, and salient" (p.102).
- *Randomized, controlled, double-blind trial*: most reliable treatment test (p.110).
- *Longitudinal studies*: same population over years; only way to know long-term effects (p.111).
Claims cited: heritability 75–80% (some >90%) (p.103); 12 risk genes found (p.103); frontal lobes etc. 3–5% smaller (p.104); sugar plays no role (p.104); 15–20% of teens/young adults with ADHD may meet gaming/Internet addiction criteria (p.104); possibly only a third lose the diagnosis by adulthood (p.111). Barkley cautions against cognitive rehabilitation apps/games and neurofeedback as for-profit claims (pp.108–109).
