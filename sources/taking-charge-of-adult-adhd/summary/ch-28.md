# Chapter 28. Driving, Health, and Lifestyle Risks
Pages 242–254.

1. **Main idea.** ADHD makes you "probability blind" (p.243): limited foresight and poor risk assessment raise driving problems, accidental injuries, health problems, and early pregnancy/STDs (pp.243–244). Barkley reports ADHD is linked to about 11–13 years of reduced life expectancy (p.244) and calls untreated adult ADHD "a public health problem" (p.244). He tells his twin brother Ron's fatal driving accident (pp.244–247).
2. **Named method/routine.** Safety strategy lists, no named system. Driving (pp.247–248): take medication on a schedule that covers your driving times (booster of immediate-release later in the day); let someone else drive if unmedicated or it may have worn off; always wear a seat belt (sticky note on dashboard or over the ignition); no alcohol when driving; stay off the phone while driving (phone-blocking app). Injuries (pp.249–250): practice stop-the-action (ch.16); take medication; use self-talk and imagery (ch.17–18); ask whether you're being rebellious; know your physical limits. Sex (pp.250–251): get educated; always use contraception unless planning a child (options discussed incl. morning-after pill, implant, vasectomy/tubal ligation); consider HPV vaccination. Health (pp.253–254): baseline physical; smoking cessation program; track alcohol; see a dentist; regular exercise; consider medication. These are the author's suggestions; consult a doctor for medical decisions.
3. **Tools/containers, one job each.** Sticky note on dashboard/ignition: remind to buckle up (p.248). Phone-blocking app: deactivate phone while moving (p.248). Small piece of paper with a check mark per drink, reviewed over a week: reveal drinking pattern (pp.253–254). Medication schedule/booster: cover driving hours (p.247–248). Free/low-cost care options for uninsured (p.254).
4. **Notes/thoughts/reference.** Drink tally on paper, looked at "over a week" (pp.253–254). Reference sites: advocatesforyouth.org; insuramatch.com app review (pp.248, 250). Otherwise not addressed.
5. **Transfers/review.** Weekly look at the drink-tally pattern (p.253–254); baseline physical then follow doctor's advice (p.253). No other review.
6. **Worksheets/deadlines.** None. Key statistics as Barkley reports: adults with ADHD ~3 times more license suspensions, 50% more crashes, ~3 times more speeding tickets (p.247); almost 50% more likely to have a serious injury; four times a serious accidental poisoning (p.249); exercise regularly 44% vs 69% (p.253).
7. **Key terms.** *Probability blind*: "you don't look ahead to consider seriously and rationally what might happen" (p.243). *Indulge* is the wrong word because it implies understanding the risk (p.243). *Conscientiousness*: personality dimension "the opposite of ADHD symptoms" linked to health and longevity (p.253).
