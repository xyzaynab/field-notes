# Chapter 30. Drugs and Crime
Pages 262–268. (Ends Step Five.)

1. **Main idea.** Adults with ADHD use substances more often than others (alcohol most; up to a third have/had a drinking problem, p.263), and hard drugs and crime track more closely with conduct disorder/antisocial behavior than with ADHD alone (pp.263, 267). Limited self-awareness makes it harder to see a "real" problem (p.263). Untreated ADHD plus substance use compounds; treat both (p.268).
2. **Named method/routine.** "Avoiding—or Getting Out of—the Trap of Drug Use" (p.268): (1) detox/rehab programs for the 20–30% with substance problems; consider tackling substance use first or simultaneously; (2) treat ADHD aggressively regardless; (3) change your environment—new friends, leisure not centered on bars; 12-step programs as a new social arena; (4) get educated/return to school. Questions to ask yourself about alcohol: worst impulsive decisions after drinking? Is drinking central to get-togethers? Does it clear your mind? (pp.264–265). Time: not stated.
3. **Tools/containers.** None for organization.
4. **Notes/thoughts/reference.** Not addressed.
5. **Transfers/review.** Not addressed.
6. **Worksheets/deadlines.** Reflection questions (above). Stats: at least one in four likely to have drug-use issues as adults (p.268); nicotine use risk nearly twice general population (p.265); Kollins 2019 study of 15,197 adolescents: smoking risk rises with each additional ADHD symptom (p.265). No deadline.
7. **Key terms.** *Self-medication* (nicotine, other stimulants) (pp.265–267). *Synergistic effect*: "more crime results in greater drug use, which in turn contributes to yet more crime" (p.267). Childhood stimulant treatment and later drug abuse: at least 16 studies found no link; "early treatment … does not contribute to a greater risk of drug abuse later in life" (p.266).
