# Chapter 17. Rule 2: See the Past … and Then the Future
Pages 163–165.

1. **Main idea.** Rule 2 "helps you see what's coming next" (p.163). After stopping the action, deliberately call up images of past similar events (hindsight) to forecast consequences (foresight), because ADHD weakens the "mind's eye" (nonverbal working memory) (pp.163–165). A lesson from last week "is likely to evaporate" before you can use it (p.163).
2. **Named method/routine.** Rule 2. Steps: (a) identify where you have the most trouble looking ahead—hammer-and-nails problem solving, slow trial-and-error learning, trouble saving/dieting/skills (p.164); (b) right after stopping the action, "click on" an imaginary visual device you find engrossing (big flat-screen TV, video game, smartphone minicam), optionally with a surreptitious physical motion while saying "Hmmmm" (pp.164–165); (c) "see for yourself, on the screen" what happened last time (p.165); (d) practise first in low-stakes situations (p.165); (e) add tangible visual aids—photo, magazine clipping, drawing of the "don't" and the "do," posted where you see them often (p.165). Time: not stated; practise often.
3. **Tools/containers, one job each.** Imaginary device (screen): displays past video (pp.164–165). Photos/clippings/drawings posted visibly: reinforce what you want vs what you've done (p.165). Example: shoe ad paired with a bank statement showing a zero balance, opposite a picture of her son and a diploma, posted inside her closet door (p.165). More on externalizing in Rule 4 (p.165).
4. **Notes/thoughts/reference.** Not addressed beyond the posted images.
5. **Transfers/review.** Not addressed; images are seen "every day" in passing (p.165).
6. **Worksheets/deadlines.** Reflection prompts (hammer/nail; trial-and-error; saving) (p.164); pointer back to ch.9 nonverbal working-memory section (p.163). No deadline.
7. **Key terms.** *Mind's eye*: nonverbal working memory (p.163). *Hindsight after the fact → self-blame* (p.163). *Visual device*: an engrossing imagined screen used to hold the past in view (p.164).
