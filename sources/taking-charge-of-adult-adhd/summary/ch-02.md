# Chapter 2. Can You Handle the Problem on Your Own?
Pages 14–18.

1. **Main idea.** Don't self-manage alone: get a professional evaluation and treatment, because self-diagnosis can miss other conditions and medication is the most effective treatment and needs a prescription (ch.2 pp.14–17).
2. **Named method/routine.** None. Seven reasons for professional help are listed (pp.14–15): rule out other conditions; detect ADHD plus another condition; get medication; access psychological therapies; learn strengths/weaknesses; get an evaluation usable for ADA accommodations; access lifestyle help (weight, smoking, alcohol/marijuana, sleep). Time: not stated.
3. **Tools/containers.** None.
4. **Notes/thoughts/reference.** Not addressed.
5. **Transfers/review.** Not addressed.
6. **Worksheets/deadlines.** None. Medication claims: fewer than 10% have no positive response to any approved drug; medication normalizes behavior of 50–65% and substantially improves another 20–30% (p.17). Adults who decline medication "typically return within 3–6 months asking to go on it" (p.17).
7. **Key terms.**
- *Comorbidity*: coexisting disorders found at evaluation (p.16). 80–85% of clinic adults have ADHD plus one other disorder; over half may have three (p.16). List of co-occurring disorders on p.16 (anxiety, ODD, CD, LDs, depression, bipolar, ASPD, addictions, tics/Tourette, autism).
