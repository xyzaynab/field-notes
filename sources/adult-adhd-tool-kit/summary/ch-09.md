# Ch 9 — Data Management: Recording, Organizing, and Managing Information  ★ priority chapter

Citation convention: EPUB has no page numbers; cites are "Ch 9, §section"; page = n/a.

## 1. Main idea
The chapter's slogan is "get it before you lose it" — and get it "in a manner that you will be able to use it later." The Daily Planner is the central place for time, tasks and key details; contacts and files go to electronic storage with backup; paper is reduced by going paperless; and assertiveness (asking for time, confirmation, e-mail summaries, saying no) is treated as an information-management skill (Ch 9, opening; §The Daily Planner, Again).

## 2. Named methods / routines
**A. Record ancillary details in the planner** (Ch 9, §The Daily Planner, Again): with a meeting, also note location and contact info, even if the number is stored elsewhere ("easy-to-access backup"). Duration: not stated.

**B. Contact capture** (Ch 9, §Electronic Backup): enter business-card/e-mail/handwritten contact info into the phone or tablet "as soon as is possible," double-check accuracy, discard the paper; update entries when details change; back up/synchronize phone to computer or cloud. Steps are prose, not numbered.

**C. Going paperless** (Ch 9, §Going Paperless): automated payments, online banking, e-readers/cloud for media, scan important papers to drives; record usernames/passwords "in a safe place." Maintenance: schedule it in the Daily Planner, "about 15 minutes or so every other week" for backing up files and scanning papers.

**D. Take Away 9.1, Assertiveness** (Ch 9, §Assertiveness and Information Management). Duration: not stated.
1. Assertiveness = ability to state a question, assertion, request or suggestion.
2. Can be cordial, professional, collaborative.
3. Identify the specific thing to express.
4. No "mind reading."
5. State it simply and straightforwardly.
6. Once said, your job is done; the other person responds.
7. Uses: request follow-up e-mail summaries after meetings; request reasonable accommodations; suggest/negotiate deadlines; ask for help (e.g., weekly progress meetings).
8. Think of other uses.

**E. Take Away 9.2, Saying No to Avoid Overcommitment** (Ch 9, §Assertiveness… / §Workplace Information). Targets "impulsive compliance."
1. Get essential information; don't assume.
2. Use a buying-time statement ("Let me think about it and get back to you"; "Give me 24 hours to check my other commitments").
3. Honestly assess feasibility; "When in doubt, say no."
4. Negotiate better terms (e.g., deadline) if possible.
5. If no, decline simply and "stay on message"; no justification needed.

**F. Take Away 9.3, Managing Information** (Ch 9, end).
1. Say "Let me get this down."
2. Use the Daily Planner or another appropriate means to record it.
3. Record accurately and completely.
4. Get confirmation of your understanding.
5. Make sure it is accessible later.
Related workplace tips: restate steps aloud while writing, summarize after recording (Ch 9, §Workplace Information).

**G. SQ4R reading strategy** (Forsyth & Forsyth, 1993; Robinson, 1970) (Ch 9, §Classroom Information): Survey (title, intro, headings, first sentences, figure titles, end summary, review questions) → Question (turn headings into questions) → Read one paragraph at a time → Record (answers/notations; in some models "reflect") → Recite (answer or summarize, aloud) → Review (go back through sections). Duration: not stated.

## 3. Tools / containers — one job each
- **Daily Planner**: "centralized organizational system"; appointments, obligations, tasks, plus contact/location details of each (Ch 9, §Planner, Again).
- **Smartphone notepad / voice recorder, tablet or laptop document, paper notebook**: overflow when the planner lacks room for detail (Ch 9, §Planner, Again).
- **Phone/tablet contact list**: durable store for contact info — "21st century version of the 'little black book'" (Ch 9, §Electronic Backup).
- **Computer/cloud sync or backup; flash drives**: protect against data loss (Ch 9, §Electronic Backup).
- **E-mail folders by project/obligation, and colored highlighting**: organize and flag messages needing follow-up (Ch 9, §Electronic Backup).
- **Manila folders or accordion files**: papers you keep instead of scanning (Ch 9, §Electronic Backup).
- **Scanner + computer/external drive**: digital copies of important papers (Ch 9, §Going Paperless).
- **Class notes**: single notebook / three-ring binder with plastic dividers per class, or separate spirals in a binder; syllabi printed into each section; or laptop/tablet with a digital folder per class, backed up (Ch 9, §Classroom).
- **Instructor's PowerPoint slides**: printed, read beforehand, annotated during lecture (Ch 9, §Classroom).
- **E-mail summary from colleague**: external reminder of agreed responsibilities (Ch 9, §Assertiveness).

## 4. Notes, thoughts, reference material
- Appointment-related information → Daily Planner (Ch 9, §Planner, Again).
- Longer details → phone notepad/voice recording, document file, or paper notebook (Ch 9, §Planner, Again).
- Contacts → phone/tablet contact list, plus backup (Ch 9, §Electronic Backup).
- Work meeting and training notes → take notes, and ask for a summary of important points (Ch 9, §Workplace Information).
- Lecture notes → class binder or per-class digital folders; syllabi with them (Ch 9, §Classroom).
- Reading notes → annotations or recorded answers via SQ4R (Ch 9, §Classroom).
- Important papers → scan to drive, or manila/accordion folders (Ch 9, §Electronic Backup, §Going Paperless).
- Passwords → "a safe place" (location unspecified) (Ch 9, §Going Paperless).
- Fleeting thoughts/ideas: **not addressed** in this chapter (the Ch 2 Comprehensive To-Do List takes "haphazard tasks and ideas").
- No single "capture" inbox or filing/retrieval system is described.

## 5. Transfers / reviews
- Paper contact info → phone contact list as soon as possible; paper discarded (Ch 9, §Electronic Backup).
- Phone data → computer/cloud via backup or sync (no frequency given for phone sync; "every other week" applies to file backups/scanning) (Ch 9, §Electronic Backup; §Going Paperless).
- Paper notes → optionally scanned to electronic versions (Ch 9, §Classroom).
- Review of agreements: notes "wear off" like a medication half-life, so "review or 'refresh'" and hold standing meetings with a supervisor or team (Ch 9, §Workplace Information). Interval not specified.
- Planner backup/review beyond Ch 4: not addressed.

## 7. Key terms (author's words)
- **Get it before you lose it** — record information in a form usable later (Ch 9, opening).
- **Daily Planner (as centralized system)** — "the go-to coping tool for all time and task management issues" (Ch 9).
- **Outsource coping / go paperless** — store things electronically as much as possible (Ch 9, §Going Paperless).
- **Assertiveness** — "the ability to state a question, assertion, request, or suggestion" (Take Away 9.1).
- **Impulsive compliance** — "the tendency to reflexively agree to do a favor or assume a responsibility when it is wiser for you to decline" (Ch 9).
- **Buying time statement** — wording that avoids an on-the-spot yes (Take Away 9.2).
- **Stay on message** — repeat your clear answer without justification (Ch 9).
- **Half-life of notes** — summaries "wear off" and must be refreshed (Ch 9).
- **SQ4R** — Survey, Question, Read, Record, Recite, Review (Ch 9).
