# Ch 17 — Your Relationship with Technology ("Good Servant, Poor Master")

Citation convention: EPUB has no page numbers; cites are "Ch 17, §section"; page = n/a.

## 1. Main idea
Technology can compensate for executive deficits but is also a constant source of distraction and escape. Since it cannot be "detoxed" away, the goal is a healthy relationship with it (like food, not like a drug), using implementation plans, stimulus control and scheduled time (Ch 17, opening).

## 2. Named methods / routines
**A. Take Away 17.1, "Turn On the Device with a Plan"** (Ch 17, §"Turn On the Device with a Plan"). Duration: not stated.
1. Specify your reason for using the device and its value.
2. Define the behavioral steps to stay on task.
3. Predict how you might get off task.
4. Devise an IF-THEN plan for the distraction.
5. Proceed, following the step-by-step plan.
Example: "open the report file, spend the first 5 minutes reviewing… look at the notes I wrote for myself last time."

**B. Recognize procrastination risks** (§Recognize Your Procrastination Risks): list your "usual suspects" and treat the urge as a signal of avoidance; the "technology distraction script."

**C. Stimulus control** (§Stimulus Control): use a device without Internet (Franzen example); turn off Wi-Fi and require manual connection; blocking/restricting apps or passwords as "artificial" impulse control.

**D. Implementation strategies for pivot points** (§Implementation Strategies for Pivot Points): task/role transitions, leaving the house, evenings/weekends, bedtime; personal rules ("no more technology after 10 p.m."; "I will not respond to any texts until after I reach the office"); technology as food sensitivity; breaks with defined start/end, a plan, an exit strategy ("X out, get water, start the smallest step"); alarms or a timer.

**E. Planned distraction time** (§Planned Distraction Time): schedule free time/"technology times" in the Daily Planner; balance with non-technology activities.

**F. Take Away 17.2, Managing Technology** (Ch 17, end):
1. Identify the problem technology habit. 2. When is it risky? 3. Old behavioral script. 4. New, realistic script and steps. 5. Why it's valuable. 6. How you might slip into the old script. 7. IF-THEN plan for drift. 8. Give it a try.
