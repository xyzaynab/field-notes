# Ch 2 — What Do You Have to Do? (The To-Do List)  ★ priority chapter

Citation convention: EPUB has no page numbers; cites are "Ch 2, §section"; page = n/a.

## 1. Main idea
Choosing and prioritizing tasks is how you "spend yourself" (time, effort, energy). The chapter splits the To-Do List into a **Comprehensive** list (a long-range "dump list" kept out of your head) and a **Daily** list (a short, disposable subset for today), both made in a protected "honest" 10 minutes of planning (Ch 2, opening; §Planning Time to Plan).

## 2. Named methods / routines
**A. Planning Time to Plan** — Take Away 2.1 (Ch 2, §Planning Time to Plan). Duration: "an honest 10 minutes — 600 seconds."
1. Devote 10 minutes (600 s) to planning.
2. Find a distraction-free place.
3. Spend the honest 10 minutes planning out your day.
4. Write your plans in your Daily Planner.
5. Define To-Do tasks in specific, behavioral terms ("do").
Advice: same place, same time daily; make it routine; adapt when traveling. Materials: paper and pen "probably the best"; word file, spreadsheet or phone notepad acceptable; "the simpler, the better."

**B. Comprehensive To-Do List** — Take Away 2.2 (Ch 2, §Comprehensive To-Do List). Duration: not stated.
1. Get a notebook or computer file devoted to it.
2. Find a distraction-free place.
3. Write down all obligations, plans, errands, commitments, recreational ideas for the next 1–6 weeks (the "dump list").
4. Store it where you can retrieve it.
5. Refer to it periodically for reminders — it is not the Daily list.

**C. Daily To-Do List** — Take Away 2.3 (Ch 2, §Daily To-Do List). Duration: 10 minutes (600 s).
1. Index card, envelope back, or other disposable paper.
2. 10 minutes (600 s) to define today's list.
3. Contents: tasks not part of your typical schedule that need special effort.
4. Max two to five items; when in doubt, fewer.
5. Define tasks in specific, behavioral terms.
6. Set a realistic time frame for each.
7. Use the Daily Planner to make an "appointment" with yourself for each task (start and end time).
8. Do each task at its time and "get it off the list."

**D. "Enter the Room with a Plan"** — Take Away 2.4 (Ch 2, §"Enter the Room…"). Duration: not stated.
1. State your reason for entering the room/desk and why it matters.
2. Define the behavioral steps to start.
3. Predict how you might go off task.
4. Write an IF-THEN plan for the barrier ("IF I encounter X, THEN I will do Y").
5. Proceed following the plan.

**E. Prioritization options** (Ch 2, §Daily To-Do List; none mandated): three-level high/medium/low, optionally color-coded green/yellow/red; or four-quadrant urgency × importance (Covey), aiming for most effort on Quadrant II (important, not urgent), the rubric used in Solanto's group CBT (2011).

## 3. Tools / containers — one job each
- **Comprehensive To-Do List** (notebook or file): enduring, externalized record of everything pending over ~1–6 weeks; stops you holding it in memory (Ch 2, §Comprehensive).
- **Daily To-Do List** (index card/envelope/phone notepad): disposable, pocketable list of 2–5 hand-picked tasks for today (Ch 2, §Daily).
- **Daily Planner**: place where tasks get times; "final step" of the To-Do domain; detailed in Ch 3 (Ch 2, §Take Away 2.3, closing).
- **Paper and pen**: preferred medium for planning (Ch 2, §Planning Time to Plan).
- **Analogy:** Comprehensive list = iTunes library; Daily list = what you download to the iPod (Ch 2).

## 4. Notes, thoughts, reference material
- Intrusive thoughts/new tasks ("popping into mind") → jotted onto the Comprehensive To-Do List, "a place to jot down and store these haphazard tasks and ideas" (Ch 2, §Comprehensive).
- Ideas with no timeline (e.g., a will, a replacement car) also go there as tasks (Ch 2, §Comprehensive).
- General notes and reference material: **not addressed** (see Ch 9).

## 5. Transfers / reviews
- Comprehensive → Daily: pick items "of most immediate importance"; tasks relevant to the next week matter most (Ch 2, §Comprehensive; iTunes analogy).
- Daily → Planner: schedule each task as an appointment (Take Away 2.3 step 7).
- Review cadence for Comprehensive list: "periodically"/"from time to time," no fixed interval; may be rebuilt from scratch, even if lost (Ch 2, §Comprehensive). Daily list is reviewed "throughout the day" (Ch 2, §Enter the Room).
- Daily list is disposable; unfinished-item handling is not stated beyond "add more after you complete these" (Ch 2, Take Away 2.3).

## 6. Worksheets / exercises / deadlines

- Kitchen example: "clean up kitchen" → "unload dishwasher and put away clean dishes" (Ch 2, §Daily).

## 7. Key terms (author's words)
- **Planning time to plan** — "a small deposit of time and effort at the outset… for larger dividends later" (Ch 2, §Planning Time to Plan).
- **Honest 10 minutes** — "a full span of 600 seconds" (Ch 2).
- **Comprehensive To-Do List** — "an all-encompassing list of your various tasks, responsibilities, and undertakings"; a "dump list" (Ch 2).
- **Daily To-Do List** — "an easy-to-access, disposable list of tasks for today" (Ch 2).
- **Specific, behavioral terms** — wording tasks as an action you can "do," the smallest manageable first step (Ch 2).
- **Make an appointment with yourself** — assign start and end times to tasks (Ch 2).
- **Enter the room with a plan** — remember your reason for entering a room/file/store so you get past distraction at the point of performance (Ch 2).
- **IF-THEN plan / implementation plan** — pre-decided response to an anticipated interruption (Gawrilow, 2011, as cited) (Ch 2).
- **Quadrant II** — high importance, low urgency (Ch 2).
