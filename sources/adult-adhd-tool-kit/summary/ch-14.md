# Ch 14 — Managing the Workplace with ADHD  ★ priority chapter

Citation convention: EPUB has no page numbers; cites are "Ch 14, §section"; page = n/a.

## 1. Main idea
Work contexts vary too much for one rule set, so the chapter covers "goodness of fit" between job demands and your abilities, how to apply the earlier foundation skills (planner, To-Do lists, recording information, assertiveness) at work, formal and informal accommodations, and whether to disclose ADHD (Ch 14, opening).

## 2. Named methods / routines
**A. Take Away 14.1, Coping with Work** (Ch 14, end of §Assertiveness and Negotiation). Duration: not stated.
1. Consider goodness of fit between you and your current or prospective job/career.
2. Where the fit is poor, determine reasonable accommodations (informal or formal).
3. Use assertiveness and negotiation on tasks and deadlines; you may make requests and proposals.
4. "Get information down before you lose it."
5. Use the Daily Planner and Daily To-Do List (and other skills) at work.

**B. Start-of-day planning** (Ch 14, §Planning, Organizing, and Getting Started): office workers spend "the first 10 minutes at the office" planning, part of it identifying and prioritizing work tasks in the Daily Planner.

**C. Block evening/weekend work time in advance** to avoid "chasing the task"; frame tasks behaviorally and time-limit them (same section).

**D. Recalibrate a fluid schedule** — a few moments to rework the schedule; challenge "Now my schedule is messed up, and I cannot recover"; the planner is the anchor (same section).

**E. Deferred-task placeholders** — put future placeholders in the planner for tasks you defer so you can "forget" them; reserve work blocks later in the week/month to do them (same section).

**F. Negotiating a deadline** — say early that Friday 5 p.m. is unrealistic and propose a specific alternative (e.g., Wednesday of next week) (Ch 14, §Assertiveness and Negotiation).

**G. Flexible hours** — request a later start/finish (e.g., 10 a.m.–6 p.m. vs. 9–5) framed as balancing life demands, with benefit to employer, monitored over "a test period of a few months"; ADHD need not be mentioned (Ch 14, §Accommodations).

**H. Disclosure formula** (Ch 14, §Do I Tell My Employer): say (1) you have ADHD and are taking steps; (2) how it makes certain tasks harder; (3) your request and plan. Sample script provided (end-of-meeting clarification; check-in the week before a due date).

**I. Implementation plan for calls** — mute phones; if not possible, an if-then rule (e.g., answer only the school or a specific customer) (Ch 14, §Accommodations).

## 3. Tools / containers — one job each
- **Daily Planner**: prioritize work tasks, hold blocks for evening/weekend work, hold placeholders for deferred tasks, anchor after disruptions (Ch 14, §Planning…).
- **Daily To-Do List**: referenced in Take Away 14.1 #5; a check-in meeting can prompt putting a task on it (Ch 14, §Accommodations).
- **Spreadsheet or white board**: externalized tracking of multiple projects (Ch 14, §Planning…).
- **Project-tracking software**: organize projects and break tasks into steps with target deadlines (Ch 14, §Accommodations).
- **Voice-activated software / assistive features**: help with reading and writing tasks (same).
- **Smartphone audio recorder, digital recorder, or notebook**: record information communicated in meetings (same).
- **Follow-up e-mail (sent or received)**: confirm and keep a record of arrangements (same).
- **Standing check-in meetings**: tangible incremental "deadlines"; make tasks salient (same).
- **Profession-specific aids**: medication-interaction app for physicians, screening checklist for a research assistant (same).
- **Work station setup**: referred to Ch 11 (not covered here).

## 4. Notes, thoughts, reference material

- Multi-project status → spreadsheet or white board (Ch 14, §Planning…).
- Deferred tasks → planner placeholders (Ch 14, §Planning…).

## 5. Transfers / reviews
- Task → planner: first 10 minutes of the workday (Ch 14, §Planning…).
- Deferred task → planner placeholder → reserved work block later in week/month (same).
- Check-in meeting in planner (e.g., Friday) → prompts mid-week work and a Daily To-Do List entry (Ch 14, §Accommodations).
- Flexible-hours arrangement is reviewed after "a few months" (same).
- Weekly or monthly review of lists: not addressed in this chapter.

## 6. Worksheets / exercises / deadlines
- No tables or worksheets.
- Decision points: job fit; whether and when to disclose; ADA disclosure "as soon as possible" if seeking formal accommodations (Ch 14, §Accommodations; §Do I Tell My Employer).
- Deadlines: trial period of a few months (flex hours); check-in "the week before" a due date (sample script).

## 7. Key terms (author's words)
- **Goodness of fit** — "the match between… the demands of the work… and… your distinct abilities and interests" (Ch 14).
- **Assertiveness / self-advocacy** — proactive asking, negotiating, requesting help (Ch 14, §Goodness of Fit; §Assertiveness).
- **Chasing the task** — working all night/weekend because work time was not set in advance (Ch 14).
- **Formal vs. informal accommodations** — ADA-protected (requires documented substantial limitation, being "otherwise qualified," and disclosure) vs. self-initiated adjustments (Ch 14, §Accommodations).
- **Magical thinking** — "If I cannot get it done by then, it will all work out, somehow" (Ch 14).
- **Shame-based reaction / debt at work** — belief you cannot ask because you have "built up a debt"; the authors reframe it as "build collateral" (Ch 14).
- **Constructive disclosure** — disclosure by an established, competent employee tied to a specific accommodation request (Ch 14).
