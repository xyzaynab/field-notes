# Ch 11 — Environmental Engineering: Managing Your Surroundings

Citation convention: EPUB has no page numbers; cites are "Ch 11, §section"; page = n/a.

## 1. Main idea
Adjust where and how you work, play and sleep so your surroundings fit ADHD: a defined work station (and a backup), stimulus control to remove temptations, workplace workarounds, and a sleep station used only for sleep (Ch 11, opening).

## 2. Named methods / routines
**A. Home work station** (Ch 11, §Your Work Station at Home): designate a space (ideally a separate room); check lighting, outlets, window/desk position, noise, distractions; respect sensitivities (white noise, headphones, earplugs, music or silence); avoid working within sight of the bed; going to the work station is itself the smallest first step ("If I simply walk over and sit down at my work station, then I can spend at least 10 minutes doing work"), described as a "launch sequence."

**B. Stimulus control** (§Stimulus Control): remove temptations ("people, places, and things"); keep entertainment items (DVDs, books) near the entertainment center, not the work station; digital distraction handled by a realistic work plan (e.g., 45 minutes), a defined computer task ("turn on the computer with a plan"), if-then plans (urge to check Facebook), and entertainment as reward.

**C. Alternative work stations** (§Alternative Work Stations): library, dorm lounge, empty classroom/office, conference room, coffee shop with Wi-Fi, relative's house.

**D. Work station at work** (§Your Work Station at Work): unused conference room; ask to close the door at set times; headphones/white noise/ear protection; work at lunch hour if needed; mute office and cell phones, log off e-mail ("allergies"); contractors use a portable hard plastic file box for invoices, later organized digitally; healthcare providers use electronic charts and reserve times for follow-up tasks.

**E. Take Away 11.1, Work Station** (Ch 11, §Your Work Station at Work):
1. Define a place for work. 2. Have a home station and an outside one (primary + backup). 3. Make sure it has minimal requirements. 4. Mind sensitivities/distractions. 5. Use stimulus control. 6. Going to your work station is often the smallest first step.

**F. Sleep station** (§Your Sleep Station): bed only for sleep and sex; keep the bed out of sight of the desk; minimize evening electronics (screen light reduces melatonin; Wood et al., 2013); curtains against sunlight; comfortable temperature; further tips in Ch 16. No numbered Take Away.

## 3. Tools / containers — one job each
- **Work station (desk/table in a defined area):** the place associated with work; the first step toward starting (Ch 11).
- **Backup work station (library, coffee shop, etc.):** alternate location (Ch 11).
- **White noise machine/app, headphones, earplugs:** block noise (Ch 11).
- **Portable hard plastic file box (contractor):** holds job invoices before digital organizing (Ch 11).
- **Smartphone/tablet/nurse-station computer:** reference tools for professionals (Ch 11).
- Daily Planner, To-Do lists: not mentioned in this chapter.

## 7. Key terms (author's words)
- **Work station** — a defined place associated with work and productivity that "primes" you (Ch 11).
- **Stimulus control** — "removing temptations in order to reduce the likelihood you will fall prey to them" (Ch 11).
- **Allergies (metaphor)** — phone, e-mail as risk factors to remove while working (Ch 11).
- **Launch sequence** — the first steps to begin a task, e.g., going to the work station (Ch 11).
- **Making a task manual** — framing the first step as a physical action ("go to work station") (Ch 11).
- **Sleep station** — bedroom arranged for sleep only (Ch 11).
