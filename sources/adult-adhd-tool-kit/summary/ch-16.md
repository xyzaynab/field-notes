# Ch 16 — Health, Well-Being, and Adult ADHD

Citation convention: EPUB has no page numbers; cites are "Ch 16, §section"; page = n/a. Medical statements below are the authors' claims, reported as such; not independently verified.

## 1. Main idea
Healthy behaviors are not an ADHD treatment, but unhealthy ones can result from and magnify ADHD, so sleep, exercise, eating, health visits, women's health, sexual health, driving and substance use are framed as targets for implementation plans (Ch 16, opening).

## 2. Named methods / routines
**A. Take Away 16.1, Sleep Routine** (Ch 16, §Sleep). Duration: bedtime routine unspecified; electronics off at least 90 minutes before bed; 1–2 week experiment.
1. Treat sleep as a priority task.
2. Define the required wake time.
3. Work backward (8–9 hours suggested in text) to bedtime; enter the sleep time in the Daily Planner.
4. Devise a wind-down routine (prepare clothes/items, electronics away 90 minutes before bed, reading or relaxing).
5. Follow sleep hygiene (caffeine cutoff, limit alcohol, bed only for sleep, no late exercise, comfortable temperature, limit naps).
6. Watch thinking errors about sleep.
7. Don't watch the clock at night.
8. If you can't return to sleep, get up for about 10 minutes to read or sit quietly.
Plus: relaxation (breathing counts, neutral image, muscle relaxation) and "get into bed time."

**B. Exercise** (§Exercise): define a realistic health behavior, a first step, and appointments with start/end times; "simple, easy, and cheap" (walking, stairs, dog walks); accountability to others (class, team, coworker); look in the planner for time; challenge "too tired" thoughts; try at least 15 minutes; habit heuristic "2 weeks — 2 work weeks and 2 weekends."

**C. Healthy eating** (§Healthy Eating): something at each of the three meal times; carry healthy snacks; hydrate; stimulus control (don't stock temptations); one implementation target — increase one healthy and decrease one unhealthy behavior (e.g., one week of apples, no chips).

**D. Monitoring health over time** (§Monitoring Your Health…): put annual/biannual visits in the Daily Planner and To-Do List; use the back page of the current planner to hold future-year appointments until the new planner; phone calendar reminders; sign up for the office's reminder systems.

**E. Women's health, motherhood, sexual health** (§Women's Health Issues… through §Sexual Health): ask prescribers/ob-gyn about medication adjustment around menstrual cycle, perimenopause/menopause; pregnancy/breastfeeding — "going professional stance" to discontinue ADHD medication (authors note limited research); ADHD associated with "four times more likely" unplanned pregnancy (cited to Barkley et al., 2008); plan for contraception that also protects against STDs.

**F. Driving** (§Driving): take medication as prescribed if driving; "enter the car with a plan" (calls/texts and GPS before driving); buffer time; call ahead if late; decatastrophize lateness; driving implementation goals (speed, yellow lights, merging); put registration, insurance, inspection, oil-change dates in the planner.

**G. Changing harmful habits** (§Changing Harmful Habits): functional assessment / reverse engineering of what a habit does for you; addiction needs treatment before ADHD treatment.

**H. Take Away 16.2, Coping with Health and Well-Being** (Ch 16, end):
1. Adequate sleep is a priority. 2. Reasonable activity as a priority task in the daily schedule. 3. At least one healthy eating habit up, one unhealthy down. 4. Use the Daily Planner to plan and monitor health behaviors. 5. Women: seek help with cycle/pregnancy/menopause changes. 6. Safe sex and birth control that also protects against STDs. 7. Safe driving (meds as prescribed; no alcohol; no texting/calls). 8. Monitor and reduce substance use including nicotine and excess caffeine.

## 3. Tools / containers — one job each
- **Daily Planner:** holds sleep time, exercise appointments, medical visits, driving admin dates; back page holds next-year appointments (Ch 16).
- **To-Do List:** used to set up visits in advance (Ch 16, §Monitoring…).
- **Phone calendar and clinic reminder systems:** backup alerts (Ch 16).
- **Healthy snack stash:** prevents hunger-driven impulsivity (Ch 16).
- **"Go-to" boring reading materials (old magazines, short-chapter books):** wind-down reading (Ch 16, §Sleep).
- **Light-adjusting apps:** referred to Appendix E (not in chapter files) (Ch 16).

## 7. Key terms (author's words)
- **Sleep as a "do"** — a targeted behavior with defined times (Ch 16).
- **Sleep script / nighttime routine** (Ch 16).
- **Night person (myth)** — grouped with "hyperfocus" and "multitasking" as romanticized ADHD myths (Ch 16).
- **Delayed Sleep Phase Syndrome** — said to affect more than half of adults with ADHD (Kooij, 2013, as cited) (Ch 16).
- **Health vs. fitness** (Ch 16, §Exercise).
- **Stimulus control (eating)** (Ch 16).
- **Enter the car with a plan** (Ch 16, §Driving).
- **Functional assessment / reverse engineering** — what purpose a harmful habit serves (Ch 16).
