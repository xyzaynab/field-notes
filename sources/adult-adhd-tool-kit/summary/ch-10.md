# Ch 10 — Materials Management: Organizing and Managing Your "Stuff"

Citation convention: EPUB has no page numbers; cites are "Ch 10, §section"; page = n/a.

## 1. Main idea
Even in a digital age there is physical "stuff" to manage. The chapter's approach: own less (ask "Do I really need this?"), give every item a dedicated place ("location, location, location"), keep a central spot for the "tools of daily life," triage mail at once, and get organized through small, specific tasks with a "good enough" system (Ch 10, opening; §Do You Need It?; §Finding a Place for Your Stuff).

## 2. Named methods / routines
**A. Declutter/acquire rules** (Ch 10, §Do You Need It?): sort clothes for items that don't fit/are worn; second pass on items unworn for 6–12 months; may take several rounds; reframe "I might need it" (items can be re-found); before buying ask "Do I need it right now?"; **24-hour delay** to "sleep on it"; consider library/used/paperback/gift/reward; **one in, one out** (discard an equivalent item for each acquired). Guidelines may be "violated."

**B. Dedicated places** (§Finding a Place): closet section, filing cabinet, plastic bin, desk drawer, smaller containers (pen cup, flash-drive box); defined spots for infrequently used items (luggage, seasonal clothing); rent or barter storage if short of space.

**C. Tools of daily life** (§Tools of Daily Life): keep keys, wallet/purse, ID, phone, glasses (plus meds, inhaler, epi-pen, book bag, laptop, charger) in a centralized spot by the front door (nail/holder, drawer, dresser container); set out gym bag etc. by the door, door-knob reminder note, or pack the car the night before; guard against coping drift ("I'll put it away later"); challenge the thought that putting things away is a hassle ("less than 30 seconds").

**D. Keeping up with mail** (§Keeping Up with Mail):
- Sort mail immediately; keep only essentials (bills, tax information, cards); discard the rest near a trash/recycling bin; OHIO — "only handle it once"; discard ads unless 100% sure of use.
- Put essentials in one container (shoebox or large manila folder): "if I received it, it is in this container."
- Process the container later the same day or weekly (e.g., Saturday morning: pay bills, file forms); put an appointment in the Daily Planner; one patient took the shoebox to a coffee shop.
- Do any online option (e.g., car registration renewal) during the mail session.
- E-mail: delete the unnecessary, tag/flag (red = time-urgent, green = personal commitments), folders (personal, bills, work), allocate time to act on flagged messages, use downtime on portable devices; use a separate e-mail address for retailers.

**E. Staying organized** (§Staying Organized): keep organizing tasks small and behavioral. **Take Away 10.2, Getting Started on Organizational Maintenance Tasks:** (1) Task-based — one item (e.g., unload bowls from dishwasher); (2) Time-based — as much as possible in a set time (e.g., 15 minutes before a meeting; "until my friend arrives"); (3) Terrain-based — one location (e.g., clear the kitchen table / desk top).

**F. Take Away 10.1, Managing "Stuff"** (Ch 10, end):
1. Specific location for tools of daily life. 2. Go paperless as feasible. 3. Define storage spaces/tools for essential paperwork. 4. Go through incoming mail daily, keep only essentials in a shoebox/container, schedule a few minutes weekly to pay bills and act. 5. A small weekly investment pays off. 6. System need only be "good enough."

## 3. Tools / containers — one job each
- **Nail/holder, drawer or dresser container by the door:** home for tools of daily life (Ch 10).
- **Shoebox or large manila folder:** holds essential incoming mail (bills, tax forms, items needing action) until the weekly session (Ch 10).
- **Trash/recycling bin next to where mail is opened:** discards non-essentials (Ch 10).
- **Daily Planner:** appointment for the mail/bill session (Ch 10).
- **E-mail flags/colors and folders; separate retailer e-mail address:** triage and keep promotions away from important mail (Ch 10).
- **Closets, filing cabinet, plastic containers, drawers, small organizers, external storage:** places for other stuff (Ch 10).

## 4. Notes, thoughts, reference material
- Reference material: scan or store digitally ("going digital," cross-ref Ch 8–9) to reduce clutter; article/book reference — digitized or re-obtainable (Ch 10, §Do You Need It?).
- Paper that requires action goes in the mail container (Ch 10).
- Notes and thoughts: **not addressed** in this chapter.

## 5. Transfers / reviews
- Incoming mail → immediate triage → essentials to container → weekly or same-day processing → pay/file/act (Ch 10, §Keeping Up with Mail; Take Away 10.1 #4).
- E-mail → flag → scheduled time to act (Ch 10).
- Clothing: 6–12-month review; multiple rounds (Ch 10).
- Purchases: 24-hour delay (Ch 10).

## 6. Worksheets / exercises / deadlines
- Take Aways 10.1 and 10.2. No tables. Deadlines: 24-hour purchase delay; weekly mail session (example Saturday morning).

## 7. Key terms (author's words)
- **Tools of daily life** — keys, wallet/purse, ID, phone, glasses and other essentials (Ch 10).
- **Location, location, location** — dedicated places for items (Ch 10).
- **OHIO** — "only handle it once" (Ch 10, §Keeping Up with Mail).
- **Coping drift** — letting small violations of a system accumulate (Ch 10).
- **Exposure/response prevention (24-hour delay)** — tolerate the urge to buy and think it through (Ch 10).
- **Task-, time-, terrain-based starts** — three ways to begin organizing (Take Away 10.2).
- **Good enough** — system need only do the job (Ch 10).
