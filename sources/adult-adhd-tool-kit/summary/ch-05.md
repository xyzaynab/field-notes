# Ch 5 — Putting the Plan into Motion (or "Once I Get Started . . .")

Citation convention: EPUB has no page numbers; cites are "Ch 5, §section"; page = n/a.

## 1. Main idea
Plans (To-Do list, planner) only matter if you start; procrastination is "the cancer of ADHD" (Ch 5, opening). The chapter gives tools for starting: define the smallest behavioral first step, use if-then implementation plans, try the 10-minute rule, spot escape behaviors, and change the thoughts and discomfort that drive avoidance.

## 2. Named methods / routines
**A. Take Away 5.1, Break Down a Task and Take the Smallest First Step** (Ch 5, §Define the Smallest Step of Behavioral Engagement). Duration: not stated.
1. Identify a task.
2. Is there a deadline or time limit?
3. Break it into steps — a "behavioral recipe" someone else could follow in the time allotted.
4. Identify steps from start to end (or work backward).
5. Make steps specific and behavioral.
6. Use the Daily Planner to budget steps across time to the deadline; make appointments.
7. Define the smallest first task, to engage or "touch" it today.
8. If still procrastinating, break that step down further.
9. Do the step — "You are no longer procrastinating."

**B. Take Away 5.2, Implementation Plans** (Ch 5, §Define the Smallest Step… after 5.1).
1. Define the smallest first step.
2. Set how long to spend; err toward less.
3. Appointment with yourself with start and end time matching #2.
4. Devise a getting-started plan using "IF X happens, THEN I will do Y."
5. Predict likely distractions/barriers.
6. Devise IF-THEN plans for those risks.
7. Do the first small step.
(Steps 4 and 6 are worded almost identically; see gaps.md.)

**C. The 10-Minute Rule / Take Away 5.3** (Ch 5, §The 10-Minute Rule). Duration: "an honest 600 seconds."
1. Define the smallest step of behavioral engagement.
2. Define the briefest time you can endure even in a worst case; recommend at least 10 minutes.
3. Clock starts when you are in position for the first step.
4. Give 10 minutes good-faith effort, then reassess.
5. "Congratulations, you did not procrastinate."
6. Decide whether to keep going or stop for now (guilt-free, an "informed decision").

**D. Take Away 5.4, Dealing with Procrastination** (Ch 5, end). Duration: not stated.
1. Identify the task. 2. Pinpoint thoughts; how you magnify negatives. 3. Label feelings ("Ugh"). 4. Highlight the task's value and how it will feel done. 5. Pinpoint positives about your ability that you minimize. 6. Think of the positive feeling on completion. 7. Break down a small first step. 8. Invest a few moments of discomfort and uncertainty to take it. 9. "You are no longer procrastinating."

**E. Other practices:** monitor how you delay tasks all day (experiment) (§Define the Smallest Step…); catch automatic thoughts by asking "What am I thinking right now?" (§Procrastination Thoughts); use escape behaviors as rewards after completing the scheduled time (§Be Aware of Your Escape Behaviors); say the first step aloud, quote or movie line, verbal commitment to someone else (§Change the Negative-to-Positive Anticipation Ratio); "scaling" and mindful acceptance of discomfort (§Identify and Accept Discomfort).

## 3. Tools / containers — one job each
- **Daily To-Do List / Daily Planner:** carry the behaviorally defined tasks and appointments; steps for a deadline budgeted into the planner (Take Away 5.1 step 6).
- **IF-THEN statements** (written plans): contingency for distraction (Ch 5, §Implementation Plans).
- **Quick written reminder to self:** parks a stray "something else I must do" thought and returns to the task (Ch 5, §Implementation Plans example). Where it is written is not specified.
- **Phone (off/away):** preventative measure against distraction (same).
- **Table 5.1 (escape behaviors list) and Table 5.2 (common justifications):** reflection prompts.

## 7. Key terms (author's words)
- **Procrastination** — "to voluntarily delay an intended course of action despite expecting to be worse off for the delay" (Steel, 2007, p. 66, quoted in Ch 5, opening).
- **Smallest step of behavioral engagement** — an action so simple "it becomes absurd to not do it" (Ch 5).
- **Touch a task** — take a necessary initial step rather than keeping it at arm's length (Ch 5).
- **Swing votes / 51–49** — you need not be fully in the mood; a slim majority is enough to start (Ch 5).
- **Implementation intention plan / if-then plan** — contingency plan to externalize executive functioning; "malware for risks to your time management plans" (Gawrilow, 2011, as cited) (Ch 5).
- **10-minute rule** — honest 600-second trial of the task, then reassess (Ch 5).
- **Escape behaviors / "usual suspects"** — activities used to avoid a task (Ch 5).
- **Procrastination script / profile** — the mix of thoughts, feelings and behaviors that delay tasks (Ch 5).
- **Automatic thoughts** — reflexive self-talk just outside awareness (Ch 5).
- **Magnification/minimization** — exaggerating negatives and discounting positives about a task (Ch 5).
- **Negative-to-positive anticipation ratio** — balance of predicted negatives vs. positives of doing a task (Ch 5).
- **"Ugh"** — minimal stress or inconvenience potent enough to cause delay (Ch 5).
- **Plea bargaining** — doing a lesser productive task to justify skipping the priority one (Ch 5).
- **Acceptance of discomfort** — tasks require some inconvenience and you can act without feeling comfortable (Hayes, 2005, as cited) (Ch 5).
