# Ch 6 — Keeping the Plan Going (Part 1): Motivation, Emotions, and Energy

Citation convention: EPUB has no page numbers; cites are "Ch 6, §section"; page = n/a.

## 1. Main idea
Most responsibilities need sustained effort, and ADHD makes it hard to keep going after a start. The chapter shows how to renew the plan, "manufacture enough motivation" by lowering task demands, rewrite avoidance "behavioral scripts," reframe time and energy, use rewards, and tolerate emotional discomfort (Ch 6, opening; §Manufacture "Enough" Motivation).

## 2. Named methods / routines
**A. Renewing the Plan** (Ch 6, §Renewing the Plan): keep priority tasks landing on the Daily To-Do List and Planner; if drifting, restart by setting a plan for the day and choosing one or two tasks with time slots. **Stopping-point note:** when wrapping up a multi-session task, plan the first step for next time and jot a few key words or a short phrase (e.g., idea for the next paragraph; "collect receipts for medical expenses" after compiling taxes). Duration: not stated.

**B. Take Away 6.1, Manufacturing (Enough) Motivation** (Ch 6, §Behavioral Scripts). Duration: not stated.
1. Define the task in specific, behavioral terms.
2. Define the smallest first step.
3. Identify negative emotional reactions that block starting.
4. Identify how you magnify negatives and minimize your ability.
5. Notice, label and accept your emotions.
6. Remember the simple steps; you need not be in the mood.
7. Take the step — no longer procrastinating.

**C. Take Away 6.2, Your Old Behavioral Script** (same section):
1. Name the new behavior plan. 2. When in the day to do it. 3. What typically happens instead? 4. List steps of the old script. 5. What do you get out of the old script?

**D. Take Away 6.3, Your New Behavioral Script** (Ch 6, §Reframe Time, after Table 6.1 discussion):
1. Develop an alternative script from the old one. 2. For each old step, a realistic incompatible step. 3. Assemble the new script. 4. Identify rewards. 5. Use other take-aways (break into steps, small first steps). 6. Follow it step by step. Worked example: Mark's "coming home" script → walk past couch/TV to the computer, open a job-search site, work at least 15 minutes, with an if-then for the urge to watch TV.

**E. Reframe Time** (Ch 6, §Reframe Time; Table 6.1): realistic start/end times (most Daily To-Do items take ≤ 1 hour); "no-lose scenario" of one hour; translate to seconds ("600 seconds"); compare with wasted time (commute, commercials, waiting rooms); use clocks/timers (analog clock, countdown timer; unplug laptop so the battery indicator becomes a timer).

**F. Take Away 6.4, Managing Your Energy** (Ch 6, §Managing Energy): six reflection questions on recharging and task order; breaks; meals/snacks; physical activity; sleep/relaxation; downtime and activities that make you feel worse.

**G. Take Away 6.5, Reward System** (Ch 6, §Setting Up Rewards): four questions on immediate and longer-term rewards, linking pleasant activities to tasks, noticing positives, converting escape activities into rewards. Premack Principle explained in text.

**H. Take Away 6.6, Managing Emotions and Tolerating Discomfort** (Ch 6, end):
1. Identify emotional reactions that contribute to avoidance. 2. Recognize the feeling (boredom, "Ugh"). 3. Rate discomfort 0–100. 4. Notice it without trying to remove it. 5. Breathe slowly and steadily. 6. Feelings need not dictate behavior. 7. You can follow through while uncomfortable. 8. Do the smallest step. 9. Observe your feelings once started. 10. Practice elsewhere.

**I. "Method acting" / acting "as if"** (Ch 6, §Accepting and Retraining Emotions): act opposite to current emotion (smile, act confident, say aloud "Okay, enough TV…").

## 3. Tools / containers — one job each
- **Daily To-Do List and Daily Planner:** keep action tasks of larger projects in view (Ch 6, §Renewing).
- **Short written note at stopping point:** hands your future self the first step; location not specified (Ch 6, §Renewing).
- **Timer / analog clock / laptop battery indicator:** make the end point visible (Ch 6, §Reframe Time).
- **Behavioral script (written step list):** replaces an automatic sequence (Ch 6, Take Aways 6.2–6.3).
- **Rewards (e.g., smoothie, song download):** follow completion of a less-liked task (Ch 6, §Setting Up Rewards).
- **Paired pleasant activity (music, TV while folding laundry):** makes chores tolerable (Ch 6, §Managing Energy).
- **0–100 discomfort scale:** keeps feelings in perspective (Take Away 6.6 step 3).

## 7. Key terms (author's words)
- **Motivation** — "the ability to generate an emotion about a task that promotes follow-through in the absence of immediate reward or consequence" (Ch 6, §Manufacture "Enough" Motivation).
- **Double whammy** — harder to generate positive emotion and harder to inhibit immediate distractions (Ch 6).
- **Food poisoning metaphor** — past aversive experiences make tasks trigger discomfort; fix by gradual "tastes" (Ch 6).
- **Lower the demands to match motivation / "How low can you go?"** (Ch 6).
- **Action often precedes motivation** (Ch 6).
- **Behavioral script** — patterned sequence of behaviors that sustains avoidance, e.g., "coming home" script (Ch 6).
- **Reframe time** — convert hours to minutes and minutes to seconds; compare with wasted time (Ch 6).
- **Premack Principle** — follow a less-desired task with a highly desired one (Ch 6, §Setting Up Rewards).
- **Emotional endurance** — tolerating short-term discomfort for long-term goals; "the only way out is through" (Ch 6).
- **Mindful acceptance** — recognize, label and endure feelings while still acting (Ch 6; cites Hayes 2005, Zylowska 2012).
- **Method acting / acting "as if"** — behaving opposite to the mood (Ch 6).
