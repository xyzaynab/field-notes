# Ch 8 — Outsourcing Coping Skills

Citation convention: EPUB has no page numbers; cites are "Ch 8, §section"; page = n/a.

## 1. Main idea
Rather than relying on coping skills every time, set up systems so recurring chores are handled without you ("trouble avoided is a problem solved"): automated payments, reminders, hired help, bartering, and accountability to others. Beware "Fool's Gold" — endlessly seeking the perfect tool (Ch 8, opening; §Find Good Tools, but Avoid "Fool's Gold").

## 2. Named methods / routines
**A. Setting up automated payments** (Ch 8, §Setting Up Automated Payments): prioritize essential utilities; options for overdraft worry — linked savings account, or a separate credit card kept off your person used only for automatic payments and paid off monthly; a separate e-mail account for automated-payment/financial messages ("automated prioritization"). Duration: "brief investment of time" (not quantified).

**B. Setting up automatic reminders** (Ch 8, §Setting Up Automatic Reminders): keep the paper planner but add smartphone/tablet alarms; pharmacy/medical-office apps and reminder texts; recurring-order and birthday/anniversary reminders; standing orders with online retailers; a visible wristwatch (analog preferred) for time monitoring.

**C. Hiring and bartering** (Ch 8, §Hiring and Bartering): hire for tasks you never get to (accountant, landscaper, handyman); investigating options becomes an item on the Comprehensive To-Do List, then broken into Daily To-Do steps; if unaffordable, barter (e.g., mowing for Wi-Fi, taxes for painting, closet swap).

**D. Outsourcing accountability** (same section): announce your plan to a friend/family, post intent and progress on social media, regular check-ins with a supervisor, professors' office hours; or state the plan aloud to yourself or a pet.

**E. Take Away 8.1, Options to Outsource Tasks** (Ch 8, §Find Good Tools…):
1. Automatic payments for recurring bills. 2. Automatic deposit of paycheck. 3. Online banking. 4. Reminders for recurring dates (birthdays, anniversaries). 5. Reminder texts/e-mails from pharmacy, medical office. 6. Hire someone for challenging tasks (tax preparation, lawn care). 7. Barter services. 8. Other options?

**F. "Simple coping is often better coping"** (same section): safe, easy, cheap — nail/hook or bowl for wallet, keys, phone; shoe box by front door for daily mail including bills; start simple and improve incrementally; "good enough."

## 3. Tools / containers — one job each
- **Automatic bill payments:** pay recurring bills without action (Ch 8).
- **Dedicated credit card (not carried):** sole payment vehicle for automatic payments; monthly statement is the record (Ch 8).
- **Separate e-mail account:** isolates payment/financial notices so any message there is likely important (Ch 8).
- **Paper Daily Planner:** primary schedule record (authors: "all things being equal, we advise using a paper Daily Planner") (Ch 8, §Setting Up Automatic Reminders).
- **Smartphone/tablet alarms and apps:** back-up reminders for recurring and infrequent items (dental visits, prescription refills, dry cleaning) (Ch 8).
- **Wristwatch (analog best), hourglass, colored/sound ADHD timers:** externalize time passing (Ch 8).
- **Comprehensive To-Do List:** holds "research accountants" type tasks (Ch 8, §Hiring).
- **Hook/bowl by the door:** holds wallet, keys, phone (Ch 8).
- **Shoe box by the front door:** holds daily mail including bills (Ch 8).

## 7. Key terms (author's words)
- **Outsourcing coping skills** — set up systems so chores "are taken care of without you having to do much" (Ch 8).
- **Fool's Gold** — seeking the perfect system, "a subtle but virulent form of procrastination" (Ch 8).
- **Pseudo-efficiency** — feeling busy on many small tasks while neglecting priorities (Ch 8).
- **Automated prioritization** — separate e-mail account so contents are likely important (Ch 8).
- **Outsourcing accountability (not responsibility)** — using others and rewards to get engaged (Ch 8).
- **Simple coping is often better coping** — safe, easy, cheap solutions (Ch 8).
- **Good enough** — judge a tool by whether it solves problems and improves functioning (Ch 8).
