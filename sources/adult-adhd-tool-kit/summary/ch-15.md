# Ch 15 — Relationships, Family, and Adult ADHD

Citation convention: EPUB has no page numbers; cites are "Ch 15, §section"; page = n/a.

## 1. Main idea
ADHD and executive dysfunction affect romantic, family, friend and work relationships (distractibility, forgetfulness, impulsive speech, broken promises). The authors note no evidence-supported couples/family model exists, so they adapt standard coping skills and relationship-enhancement strategies, focusing on committed partnerships (Ch 15, opening).

## 2. Named methods / routines
**A. Take Away 15.1, Coping with ADHD-Affected Relationships** (Ch 15, end). Duration: check-ins at least 10 minutes.
1. Confirm diagnosis; individual treatment for the partner with ADHD.
2. Regular check-in times: at least 10 minutes, sitting together, face-to-face, no interruptions (children, phones, TV).
3. Use check-ins for daily-life business and to arrange positive time.
4. Use empathy and communication skills to manage emotions.
5. Couples therapy with an ADHD-familiar clinician can help.

**B. Scheduling important conversations** (§Scheduling Times for Important Conversations): reserve a protected time and place; prepare coping reminders (listen without interrupting, repeat back, clarify, convert feedback to behavioral terms); ask for a time out if overwhelmed.

**C. Regular check-ins** (§Scheduling Regular Check-In Meetings): first homework for ADHD-affected couples — at least two times in the coming week, face-to-face, full attention, no distractions; builds toward finances/parenting and light conversation; may later be done on walks or in the car.

**D. Empathy and communication skills** (§Empathy Behaviors and Emotional Regulation): summarizing ("So what you are saying is…"); disarming (find a grain of truth); thought/feeling empathy; inquiry ("Is there anything else I'm missing?"); "I" statements; **XYZ strategy** — "When X happens, I feel Y, and I would like us to be able to Z."

**E. Handling emotions during interactions** (§Handling Emotions…): (1) define your role (e.g., "an employee listening to what my boss has to say"); (2) listen without interrupting; notice feelings without acting; breathe; relax muscles; use empathy statements; (3) take time afterward to process, then return to the role to set an action plan.

**F. Implementation plans for predictable settings** (§Implementation Plans…): define your role and what not to say ahead of meetings, dinners, gatherings; **three-sentence rule** (Rosenfield, Ramsay & Rostain, 2008) — pause after three sentences; parenting: speak quieter, avoid or accommodate predictable triggers, distraction, rewards, "exit strategy."

**G. Assertiveness and negotiation** (§Assertiveness…): challenge "social debt" thinking (mind reading + overgeneralization); ask for favors and help, including small requests.

**H. Externalized coping reminders** (§Externalized Coping Reminders): index-card "coping cards" or "cheat sheets" taken into meetings/check-ins ("Ask for clarification about the deadline…"; "No jokes during this meeting"; "Don't do others' thinking for them").

## 3. Tools / containers — one job each
- **Daily To-Do List:** a promise to a friend is a prioritized task (Ch 15, §Committing to Treatment).
- **Daily Planner:** errands promised to a spouse are scheduled in it (Ch 15, same section).
- **Check-in meeting:** regular face-to-face coordination of household business (Ch 15).
- **Coping card / index card / tablet of paper:** in-the-moment reminders of do's and don'ts (Ch 15).
- **Basket by the front door:** holds keys and phone; partner cooperation needed (Ch 15, §Handling Emotions…).

## 7. Key terms (author's words)
- **Social collateral** — gained by delivering on promises and projects (Ch 15, opening).
- **Social debt** — "the balance of favors you owe others and favors owed to you" (Ch 15).
- **Mind reading** — assuming you "just know" another's thoughts; most common relationship distortion (Ch 15).
- **Disarming** — finding a grain of truth in criticism (Ch 15).
- **Summarizing** — stating back the main points (Ch 15).
- **XYZ strategy** — feedback format (Ch 15).
- **Role definition** — naming your job in an interaction to create a behavioral script (Ch 15).
- **Three-sentence rule** — pause after three sentences (Ch 15).
- **Check-in** — regular protected conversation to prevent issues (Ch 15).
- **Emotion management (not "emotion solving")** (Ch 15).
- **Coping cards** — index-card reminders (Ch 15).
