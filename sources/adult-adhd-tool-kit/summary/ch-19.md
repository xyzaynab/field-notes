# Ch 19 — The Role of Medications in the Treatment of Adult ADHD

Citation convention: EPUB has no page numbers; cites are "Ch 19, §section"; page = n/a. Clinical statements are the authors' claims, reported as such; not independently verified.

## 1. Main idea
CBT targets how ADHD symptoms affect functioning; medications target the symptoms themselves. Combined treatment "usually produces the best results" ("pills don't teach skills"). The chapter covers why consider medication, drug types and mechanisms, monitoring, side effects, risks, comorbidity, substance use, misuse, and attitudes (Ch 19, opening; §Why Should I Consider Medications…; §Additional Advice…).

## 2. Named methods / routines
No numbered "Take Away" in this chapter. Procedural content:
- **Five ways to tell if medication is working** (Ch 19, §What Can I Expect…): (1) easier to start/finish/focus; (2) measure a specific target behavior before/after (e.g., time reading uninterrupted); (3) clinician's symptom questionnaire at each visit; (4) feedback from significant others; (5) optional computerized attention/impulse test.
- **Onset timing:** stimulants immediate; alpha agonists a few days; atomoxetine up to 8 weeks (same section).
- **Dosing rules:** take as prescribed; atomoxetine and alpha agonists daily; stimulants usually daily, flexible if sensible; always take for driving; alpha agonists must be weaned gradually (§Do I Have to Take Medications Every Day?; §Will I Have to Take Medications for the Rest of My Life?).
- **Monitoring cardiac health:** vital signs, weight and cardiac symptoms measured regularly after starting (§Are There Any Dangers?).
- **Substance-use plan:** reduction of substance use as a treatment goal; choose lower-abuse-potential options (§What If I Have Problems with Substance Abuse?).
- **Ground rules for stimulant use:** scheduling, monitoring, refills; lost prescriptions are a "red flag" (§Additional Advice).

## 3. Tools / containers — one job each
- **Stimulants (amphetamine-derived; methylphenidate-derived):** short-acting symptom reduction (Ch 19, §What Are the Medications…).
- **Nonstimulants (atomoxetine; guanfacine; clonidine):** 24-hour coverage; "signal boosters" for the alpha agonists (same).
- **Symptom questionnaires and behavior measures:** assess effect (Ch 19).
- **Figure 19.1:** diagram of stimulant action at the dopamine synapse (amphetamine: releases transmitter from vesicle, blocks reuptake, inhibits MAO; methylphenidate: blocks reuptake into the presynaptic terminal) (Ch 19, Images/3.png).

## 5. Transfers / reviews

## 7. Key terms (author's words)
- **Bottom up vs. top down** — medications vs. psychosocial treatment (Ch 19).
- **Bio-psycho-social approach** (Ch 19).
- **Misuse / diversion / abuse / catastrophic use** — defined in §Additional Advice (Ch 19).
- **Rebound hyperactivity** — symptoms return as medication wears off (Ch 19).
- **Magical thinking about medications** — unrealistic expectations (Ch 19).
- **"Pills don't teach skills"** (Ch 19).
- **Individual agency** — self-directed activity, enhanced but not determined by medication (Ch 19).
