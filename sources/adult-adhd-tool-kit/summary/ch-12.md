## 1. Main idea
Problem solving and decision making are "tests" of executive function. The authors prefer "problem management" because not all problems can be solved as wished. Two parallel five-step templates externalize the process, and reframes address avoidance, tie-breaking and uncertainty (Ch 12, opening).

## 2. Named methods / routines
**A. Take Away 12.1, Problem Management Template** (Ch 12, §Problem Management Template; summary box at §Decision-Making Template end). Duration: not stated.
1. Define the problem in specific, behavioral terms (it may contain several nested problems or a decision).
2. Brainstorm as many options as possible without editing.
3. Assess pros, cons and feasibility; rank from most to least promising.
4. Implement the best option (may not be the easiest).
5. Assess the outcome; if the problem persists, reenter at Step 1.
Also: define the course of action in strict behavioral terms as a "problem management script" (e.g., e-mail the professor).

**B. Take Away 12.2, Decision-Making Template** (§Decision-Making Template):
1. Define the decision in specific, behavioral terms (with limiting parameters).
2. Identify options.
3. Weigh pros and cons; consider whether more information is needed.
4. Select and commit ("Live into a good decision").
5. Assess the outcome; reenter if needed.

**C. Tie-break reframes** (§Decision-Making Template): "no way to make a wrong decision" given due diligence; rewind thought experiment; coin-flip test — "how would I be rooting for it to land?"

**D. Two levels of decision making** (§Facing Uncertainty): (1) best decision from information available; (2) "living the choice into being a good decision" by implementing fully (Patton: "Make a decision and then do it like hell").

## 7. Key terms (author's words)
- **Problem management** — preferred over "problem solving" because not all problems can be solved as hoped (Ch 12).
- **Problem management script** — the course of action in strict behavioral terms (Ch 12).
- **Passive resignation** — belief that nothing can be done, e.g., "I deserve whatever I get" (Ch 12).
- **Pocket veto** — passive non-decision that lets an option lapse (Ch 12, §Facing Uncertainty).
- **Living the choice into being a good decision** — commit and make it work (Ch 12).
- **Accepting emotional discomfort** — facing problems despite it (Ch 12).
