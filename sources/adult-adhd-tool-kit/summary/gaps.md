# Gaps, pointers without explanations, and inconsistencies

Source: Ramsay & Rostain, *The Adult ADHD Tool Kit* (EPUB). Cites are by chapter and section; the EPUB has no page numbers, so print pages are **unverified** throughout.

## A. Problems with the source file (not the book's fault)
1. **No page numbers.** Every "chapter and page" cite in this set is chapter + section only. (EPUB: no pagebreak markers found in any chapter file.)
2. **Chapter 1 opens mid-passage.** The extracted text begins "For example, many adults with ADHD aim to reduce procrastination…" and later says "Fourth and finally…" with the first three points missing (Ch 1, opening). Treat Ch 1 as incomplete.
3. **Chapter 1's promised take-aways are absent.** It ends "let us propose the first take away points" but no box follows, and the table of contents lists no Take Away 1.x (Ch 1, end; TOC).
4. **Appendices not in the file.** Appendices B, D, E and F are cited (Ch 8 reminder apps and Ch 16 light-adjusting apps → Appendix E; Ch 19 → Appendix F; Ch 20 → Appendix D; Ch 16 → Appendix B) but none is in the EPUB chapter files. Contents unverified.
5. **Tables 7.2/7.3 are images**, and the second example row of Table 7.3 is cut off at the image edge (Ch 7). Figure 19.1 is also an image (Ch 19).
6. **Table of contents is inconsistent with the text.** It lists Take Aways 6.1, 6.3 and 6.4 but the text also has 6.2 ("Your Old Behavioral Script"); it places Table 6.1 after 6.3 text; it lists Table 5.2 as a sentence (Ch 6, Ch 5; TOC).
7. **Stray characters** (e.g., a lone "2" before Ch 10's title) are extraction artifacts.

## B. Things the book points to but does not explain
1. **No single capture-and-retrieve system for notes, ideas and reference material.** The book offers separate pieces: random tasks/ideas → Comprehensive To-Do List (Ch 2); "a quick reminder to myself" (Ch 5, §Implementation Plans) and "a couple key words" at a stopping point (Ch 6, §Renewing the Plan) with **no destination named**; overflow detail → phone notepad, voice recorder, document or paper notebook (Ch 9); mail → shoebox (Ch 10); papers → manila/accordion folders or scans (Ch 9). No indexing, tagging, filing hierarchy or retrieval routine for notes is described. Reference material beyond "scan it" and "digital folders per class" is not addressed.
2. **No weekly or monthly review routine.** The planner is reviewed at the start/end of day and glanced at through the day (Ch 4, Take Away 4.1). The Comprehensive To-Do List is reviewed "periodically" or "from time to time" with no interval (Ch 2, §Comprehensive). A weekly mail/bill session is the only named weekly routine (Ch 10). A maintenance block of "15 minutes or so every other week" applies only to file backup/scanning (Ch 9).
3. **How items leave the Comprehensive list** is described only by analogy (iTunes library → iPod download, Ch 2); there is no rule for choosing, deferring or deleting items, or for what to do with unfinished Daily List items (Ch 2, Take Away 2.3).
4. **Where passwords are kept:** "a safe place" (Ch 9, §Going Paperless). No further guidance.
5. **Where the paper planner lives** between uses is not stated; Ch 3 and Ch 4 call it a "tool of daily life" with keys and wallet, but Ch 10's list of tools of daily life (keys, wallet/purse, ID, phone, glasses, medications, book bag, laptop) omits it (Ch 3 Take Away 3.1 #7; Ch 10, §Tools of Daily Life).
6. **Tracking tools for multiple projects** — "software programs that help with organizing and tracking projects" are mentioned without names (Ch 14, §Accommodations); spreadsheet or white board are the only named options (Ch 14, §Planning…).
7. **"Wearing apps" and "specialized apps"** (Ch 8, Ch 16) are deferred to Appendix E (see A4).
8. **Sources cited as "in press" or without full detail** (e.g., Ramsay, in press, Ch 15; "over 20 clinical outcome studies," Ch 1) — claims unverified.
9. **Ch 16 clinical and statistical claims** (e.g., "four times more likely to have an unplanned pregnancy"; untreated-ADHD driving compared to intoxication; "more than half… Delayed Sleep Phase Syndrome") rest on cited sources not included in the EPUB; unverified here.

## C. Places where the book is unclear or inconsistent
1. **Is the "10 minutes" one session or several?** Take Away 2.1 sets an honest 10 minutes to plan the day (and write plans in the Daily Planner, step 4); Take Away 2.3 sets another 10 minutes to define the Daily To-Do List; Take Away 4.2 sets at least 10 minutes to plan the day in the planner; Ch 14 says the first 10 minutes at the office; Table 4.2 shows "9–9:10 Plan for day." The book never says these are the same block (Ch 2, Ch 4, Ch 14).
2. **Order of Daily To-Do List vs. planner entry:** Take Away 2.1 step 4 writes plans in the planner; Take Away 2.3 builds the list first and then schedules it in the planner (step 7); Ch 3 says entering the Daily To-Do List into the planner is cognitive rehearsal. Which comes first is unclear (Ch 2, Ch 3).
3. **When to plan:** "at the beginning or end of their day (or both)" (Ch 4, §Review the Daily Planner) vs. Take Away 4.1 "start of your day (or night before)" — "both" is not reflected in the Take Away.
4. **Paper vs. digital:** Ch 3 recommends paper ("not a forever decision"); Ch 8 repeats "all things being equal… paper" but recommends adding electronic alarms; Ch 17 treats digital as backups. Not a contradiction, but the rule for which system is the source of truth when both exist is never stated (Ch 3, 8, 17).
5. **Take Away 5.2 steps 4 and 6** repeat the same IF-THEN instruction; step 4 is meant to be a getting-started plan and step 6 a plan for distractions but both are worded identically (Ch 5, Take Away 5.2).
6. **Table 4.1 schedule:** the report is "due in two weeks" but steps 6 and 10 both say "Monday at 9 a.m." and steps 4–5 say "Friday"/"Saturday" without dates; which Monday is which is implicit (Ch 4, Table 4.1).
7. **Sleep length:** "8 or 9 hours" is recommended while acknowledging 7 hours may suffice for some; no way to determine individual need is given (Ch 16, §Sleep).
8. **Check-in frequency/length:** the text says at least two times in the coming week; only Take Away 15.1 says "at least 10 minutes" (Ch 15).
9. **Medication guidance on stimulants:** Ch 19 says taking stimulants daily is "usually advisable," while Ch 16 notes their short half-life lets you take them only when you "need it" and warns of underestimating need (Ch 19, §Do I Have to Take Medications Every Day?; Ch 16, §Driving). Both are consistent on driving.
10. **Pregnancy/ADHD medication:** "no existing research evidence" of fetal risk yet "the going professional stance" is to discontinue (Ch 16, §Women's Health Issues). The book acknowledges this tension itself.
11. **Duplicated material:** boat-owner rule (Ch 4 and Ch 18), SQ4R (Ch 9 and Ch 13), 10-minute-rule time reframing (Ch 5 and Ch 6) — not errors, but cross-references vary ("Chapter 4" for the planning 10 minutes in Ch 6 though Ch 2 introduces them).
