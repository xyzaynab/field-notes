# Ch 4 — Define, Prioritize, and Choreograph What You Do (Time and Task Management)  ★ priority chapter

Citation convention: EPUB has no page numbers; cites are "Ch 4, §section"; page = n/a.

## 1. Main idea
Having a Comprehensive list, a Daily list and a planner gives you the pieces; this chapter is "how you play the game": reviewing the planner often, entering every obligation, breaking tasks into behavioral first steps, building in buffers, scheduling downtime, and sequencing tasks around effort and energy (Ch 4, opening).

## 2. Named methods / routines
**A. Reviewing Your Daily Planner** — Take Away 4.1 (Ch 4, §Write Down All of Your Existing Obligations). Duration: not stated per glance; the planning block is 10 min.
1. Review the planner at the start of the day (or the night before).
2. Glance at, review and "refresh" it through the day.
3. If your situation changes often, set fixed times to adjust the planner.
4. Record new future commitments as they arise.
5. Use the planner to track what you completed, in addition to checking off the Daily To-Do List.
6. Repeat.

**B. Using Your Daily Planner** — Take Away 4.2 (same section). Duration: at least 10 min (600 s).
1. Take the planner to a reduced-distraction setting.
2. Devote at least 10 minutes (600 s) to planning the day.
3. Review obligations already recorded.
4. Record known commitments (meetings, classes, school pick-up/drop-off).
5. Reserve times for self-care (sleep, meals, exercise).
6. Leave buffer time between tasks for breaks, commuting.
7. Make appointments for Daily To-Do List tasks.
8. Make sure those tasks are in reasonable, behavioral terms.
9. Schedule downtime and recreational/social activities.
10. Check the order of tasks is sensible and realistic.
11. "Trust the plan" — engage in the first task, one step at a time.
12. Refer to the planner frequently through the day.
Timing guidance: a 10-minute block "either at the beginning or end of their day (or both)" (Ch 4, §Review the Daily Planner—Early and Often).

**C. Define Tasks in Behavioral Terms** — Take Away 4.3 (Ch 4, §Breaking Down a Task). Duration: not stated.
1. Look at Daily To-Do List tasks.
2. Spot ones that are overwhelming or too broad ("write paper," "clean house").
3. Reword as a smaller, specific, doable step ("reread last paragraph," "unload dishwasher").
4. Repeat step 3 until you are confident you will do it.
5. Put the redefined task on the Daily To-Do List.
Example: "organize my room" → remove clutter / decide what stays / decide where items are stored → first item "pick up any dishes… take them to the kitchen" (Ch 4, §Breaking Down).

**D. Other named tactics:** write all existing obligations in first, including routine ones, travel, wake-up routine, meals, chores and sleep (budget of 24 hours; obligations are "standard expenses") (§Write Down…); buffer times and the "60 mile-per-hour planning error" (§Commuting and Other Buffer Times); boat-owner rule "double the number and add an increment of time" (not literal) and timing tasks with a timer to learn actual duration (same section); schedule unstructured/weekend/work-from-home time and downtime (§Scheduling Unstructured…); choreography/sequencing, e.g., exercise first vs. low-priority "warm-up" tasks, pivot points like mail on arriving home or 15 minutes of e-mail after lunch (§Choreography); adjust when life happens and ask whether a surprise was truly unexpected or caused by procrastination (§Make Adjustments); "Do It Now" — focus on the task due now and "strategically forget" later ones because they are recorded (§Do It Now).

## 3. Tools / containers — one job each
- **Daily Planner:** external map of the day; the "budget" of 24 hours; record of what was completed (Ch 4, §Write Down…; Take Away 4.1 step 5).
- **Daily To-Do List:** portable priority tasks, the "street level view" while the planner is the wide Google Maps view (Ch 4, §Write Down…).
- **Comprehensive To-Do List:** named only in the opening as one of the basic tools; no new job (Ch 4, opening).
- **Spreadsheet / sheet of paper / index cards:** lay out a large project's start, end and in-between steps (Ch 4, §Breaking Down).
- **Timer:** measure how long routine tasks actually take (Ch 4, §Commuting and Other Buffer Times).

## 4. Notes, thoughts, reference material
Not addressed, apart from using the planner to record appointments and commitments as they arise (Take Away 4.1 step 4). Recording of project notes/information is Ch 9.

## 5. Transfers / reviews
- Daily To-Do List task → planner slot with a time (Take Away 4.2 step 7); routine obligations (e.g., standing class) go in the planner only, not on the To-Do List (Ch 4, §Write Down…).
- New commitments go straight into the planner (Take Away 4.1 step 4).
- Review: start of day or night before; glances through the day; set adjustment times if plans change frequently (Take Away 4.1). Completed tasks are checked off the To-Do List (Take Away 4.1 step 5).
- Weekly or monthly review of the planner or Comprehensive list: not addressed in this chapter.

## 7. Key terms (author's words)
- **Time management "moves"** — tactics for combining tools to follow through (Ch 4, opening).
- **Planner as budget** — "a financial budget with a daily account of 24 hours to be 'spent'" (Ch 4).
- **Existing obligations** — "standard expenses… deducted from your budget" (Ch 4).
- **Zeno's paradox (applied)** — any task can be divided into ever-smaller steps; the right first step gives "I can do this" (Ch 4, §Breaking Down).
- **Smallest behavioral first step** — e.g., open the file, pick up the textbook (Ch 4).
- **Buffer time / "60 mile-per-hour" error** — assuming no friction in travel time (Ch 4).
- **Recommended daily allowance** — balanced schedule includes work plus exercise, hobbies, downtime (Ch 4).
- **Choreography** — sequencing and ordering tasks to manage effort and energy, "Apollo 13" metaphor (Ch 4).
- **Anchor points / pivot points** — fixed points that help pace the day (Ch 4).
- **Do it now** — see §2D.
