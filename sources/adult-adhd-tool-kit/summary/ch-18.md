# Ch 18 — Committing to Undoing the Effects of Adult ADHD

Citation convention: EPUB has no page numbers; cites are "Ch 18, §section"; page = n/a.

## 1. Main idea
Skills must be maintained long term like exercise. Slipping ("coping drift") is certain ("relapse rate… is 100%"); what matters is how you handle it. The chapter treats ADHD management as a lifestyle change comparable to diabetes care, and advises patience, "start small," and trust in the plan (Ch 18, §Dealing with "Coping Drift"; §Coping with ADHD as a Lifestyle Change).

## 2. Named methods / routines
**A. Dealing with coping drift** (Ch 18, §Dealing with "Coping Drift"), steps in prose:
1. Reverse engineer the drift from the present back to where it started; identify the behavioral script you fell into and design an alternative.
2. Define precisely the behavior to implement and why it is worthwhile.
Worked example: John's lateness traced to dropping the evening coffeemaker/timer routine (coffee-shop stop + snooze).

**B. Shifting priorities** (§Shifting Priorities): drift may reflect changed priorities (informed decision to revise a goal) or too many simultaneous changes (go back to "start small"); "focus on what is wrong with the plan, not what is wrong with you."

**C. Paying down debt / building collateral** (§Paying Down Debt…): persistent skill use as deposits; "wake effect" — others take months to update their opinion (de Boo & Prins, 2007, as cited); credit yourself; therapist support.

**D. Trust the plan** (§Trust the Plan…): notice daily small successes; focus on "flowers as much as weeds"; Tour de France metaphor — "Just keep pedaling."

**E. Table 18.1, General Coping Reminders** (Ch 18, end). Ten reminders: "It doesn't have to be fun to be fun"; "Leave room for cream"; "Swing votes"; "Choreography"; "Allergy/food sensitivity"; "Turnaround time" (from *Into Thin Air*); "Boat owner's advice"; "Right now I'm feeling $30,000 of psychoanalysis dialing 9-1-1"; "If you invented a medication that created this degree of change, you'd be a millionaire"; "Recycle cans, not can'ts."
