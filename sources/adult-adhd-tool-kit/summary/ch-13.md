# Ch 13 — Managing College with ADHD

Citation convention: EPUB has no page numbers; cites are "Ch 13, §section"; page = n/a.

## 1. Main idea
College is "as rigorous a test of your executive functioning and motivational skills as it is of your academic abilities." The chapter covers readiness and fit, accommodations (ADA/OSD), dorm and study-time management, reading/writing/math strategies, self-advocacy, self-care and technology; it applies to other adult education too (Ch 13, opening).

## 2. Named methods / routines
**A. Readiness and set-up before arrival** (Ch 13, §Readiness…): consider community college first or during academic leave; weigh school size/class size; research Office of Student Disabilities (OSD), learning and counseling centers; secure ADA documentation (psycho-educational testing within the past 3 years; diagnosis alone insufficient); apply early — "takes time… not something that can be completed a few days before an exam"; book clinical appointments ideally the week before the semester ("more akin to scheduling a surgery than a haircut"); consider lowered course load or longer timeline.

**B. Reserving time for studying** (§Reserving Time for Studying): fill the planner with all weekly commitments; find gaps; set start and end (e.g., "10:30 a.m. to 12 noon"); name a behavioral task for the block ("work on problem set… easiest problem first"); find your minimum block (~30 minutes) and upper limit (~2 hours, "point of diminishing returns"); use an if-then plan for invitations; try the planner simply to log how time is spent.

**C. Take Away 13.1, SQ4R Technique for Reading** (Ch 13, §Reading, Writing, and Arithmetic; repeats Ch 9):
1. Survey headings, bold terms, illustrations. 2. Develop questions. 3. Actively read each section, making notes on the page or elsewhere. 4. Take notes in your own words to answer questions. 5. Recite answers and summary. 6. Review headings, answers, notes.

**D. Take Away 13.2, Writing Papers** (same section):
1. Read the assignment, specifications, due date.
2. Break down tasks (research, reading, outlining, drafts, intervening due dates).
3. Work backward from the due date and define times.
4. Start by thinking/outlining (ideas, index cards, or PowerPoint-style bullet points).
5. Follow the outline; first draft without editing.
6. Return later to clarify expression (a separate task).
7. Lower the bar: meet minimum requirements, not an "A" paper.
8. Trust the plan.
Also: avoid pseudo-efficiency (e.g., endless article downloading).

**E. Math** (same section): use SQ4R on the textbook, search concepts online (e.g., videos), keep working problem sets, skip to the next problem if stuck, record examples in class, seek instructor/learning center/tutor help.

**F. Asking for help** (§Asking for Help and Self-advocacy): office hours, review sessions, TAs, advisors, OSD, learning center, treating clinicians; define the contact step behaviorally ("contact my professor and explain the situation"); neutralize what-if thoughts with the Defense Attorney; "invest 15 seconds of discomfort."

**G. Dorm life** (§Dorm Life): environmental engineering; use the campus library if the room is a problematic "behavioral script"; petition for a single room if needed.

**H. Technology** (§Dealing with Technology): e.g., turn off phone and Wi-Fi in class, reconnect on a planned break (see Ch 17).

## 3. Tools / containers — one job each
- **Daily Planner:** all weekly commitments and study blocks with start/end times (Ch 13).
- **Daily To-Do List:** named as vital for studying (Ch 13).
- **Notes on page / elsewhere; own-words notes:** SQ4R reading notes (Take Away 13.1).
- **Index cards or PowerPoint-style bullet outline:** hold and reorder paper ideas (Take Away 13.2 #4).
- **Syllabus:** source of the assignment parameters and due dates (Ch 13, §Reading, Writing…; Take Away 13.2 #1).
- **OSD documentation/accommodations:** extended exam time, reduced-distraction exams, priority registration (Ch 13).
- Class notes binder system: covered in Ch 9, not repeated here.

## 4. Notes, thoughts, reference material
- Reading notes (own words) in margins or elsewhere (Take Away 13.1). Paper ideas on index cards, bullets or outline (Take Away 13.2). Sources already collected: write from them instead of continuing to download (Ch 13).
- Class notes and syllabi filing: see Ch 9; not addressed here.

## 5. Transfers / reviews
- Final due date → backward-planned tasks → planner blocks (Take Away 13.2 #2–3).
- Outline/index cards → draft → separate editing pass (Take Away 13.2 #4–6).
- SQ4R: review pass after reading (Take Away 13.1 #6).
- Planner as time log reviewed to sort social/academic blocks (Ch 13, §Reserving Time for Studying).

## 6. Worksheets / exercises / deadlines
- Take Aways 13.1, 13.2. Deadlines: ADA documentation test within the past 3 years; accommodation request well before exams; clinical appointments ideally the week before term; paper due dates and intermediate due dates (topic approval, draft) (Ch 13).

## 7. Key terms (author's words)
- **Goodness of fit (college)** — match between you and campus/size/resources (Ch 13).
- **Witness Relocation Program analogy** — moving away strips scaffolding and "collateral" built in high school (Ch 13).
- **FOMO** — "fear of missing out" (Ch 13).
- **Deferred gratification** — study now, social later (Ch 13).
- **Point of diminishing returns** — upper limit of a study block (Ch 13).
- **Pseudo-efficiency** — busy with paper-related tasks that aren't writing (Ch 13; see Ch 8).
- **Self-advocacy** — reaching out because faculty won't notice a problem unprompted (Ch 13).
- **Reasonable accommodation** — e.g., single room, extended time (Ch 13).
