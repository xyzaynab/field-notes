# Front matter (Preface; "Why Should I Expect…"; "A Note for Mental Health Clinicians")

Not a numbered chapter; included so Ch 1 can be read in context. Cites are by section; page = n/a.

- **Premise:** ADHD is "a disorder related to poor self-regulation"; people "know" what to do but cannot execute at the "point of performance" (Preface). CBT's cognitive part addresses pessimistic thoughts that arise from living with ADHD; the behavioral part builds a coping repertoire (Preface).
- **Medication vs. CBT:** medication is compared to corrective eyeglasses; it "reduces the symptoms" but "you will not spontaneously complete your income taxes" (Preface).
- **Take Away points:** written lists to "refer to or carry with you"; "behavioral prescriptions" for the 167 hours outside the weekly therapy hour (Preface). The clinician note says they can be copied as coping cards (Clinicians).
- **Reading order:** "readers are invited to jump to chapters relevant to their specific coping issues and need not read the book in order" (Preface).
- **Why trust it:** the skills overlap other books; repetition helps skills "stick"; the authors say they are "good at helping adults with ADHD understand how they do not do things they set out to do" (Why Should I Expect…).
- **Companion text:** Ramsay & Rostain (2015), *Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Adult ADHD*, second edition (Clinicians).
- **Progress reframe:** 50% → 80% on-time assignments is "30%… we'd be billionaires" (Preface).
