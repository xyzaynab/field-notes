# Ch 7 — Keeping the Plan Going (Part 2): Attitudes, Beliefs, and Self-Esteem

Citation convention: EPUB has no page numbers; cites are "Ch 7, §section"; page = n/a.

## 1. Main idea
ADHD is not caused by distorted thinking, but years of frustration breed pessimistic attitudes and avoidance ("invisible fences"). Catching and challenging automatic thoughts, recommitting to plans, acting "as if," and revising core beliefs are treated as necessary parts of keeping coping skills in use (Ch 7, opening).

## 2. Named methods / routines
**A. Take Away 7.1, Catching Automatic Thoughts** (Ch 7, §Catching Your Automatic Thoughts). Duration: not stated.
1. Use changes in feeling or avoiding a task as signs of negative automatic thoughts.
2. Work out the triggering situation.
3. What was the thought/interpretation?
4. How does it influence feelings and behavior?
5. Any thinking errors? (see Table 7.1)

**B. Take Away 7.2, Changing Automatic Thoughts Using Your Defense Attorney** (Ch 7, §Changing Your Thoughts) (from Freeman & Reinecke, 1993):
1. Recognize automatic thoughts about the task. 2. Treat them as a Prosecutor's case before a judge and jury. 3. Have the Defense Attorney object to errors, missing information, exaggeration and argue a balanced view. 4. Weigh evidence and plan action from the adaptive view. 5. Use earlier take-aways to get engaged.
Supporting prompts (five questions: advise-a-friend; another way to think; do I already know what to do; can I accept imperfection; grand scheme) (same section).

**C. Take Away 7.3, Developing Alternative Thoughts** (Ch 7, §Method Acting Approach):
1. What am I thinking; thinking errors? 2. Another way; what would the Defense Attorney say? 3. Worst, best, most likely outcome. 4. Specific steps to influence it. 5. What would I tell a friend? 6. Significance in an hour/day/month/year. 7. A small step for later satisfaction.
Thought-change record: Table 7.2 is a blank five-column form — Triggering Situation | Prosecutor Attorney Thoughts | Reactions (feelings, behaviors, etc.) | Defense Attorney Thoughts | Action Plan. Table 7.3 shows it filled in (e.g., trigger "I am putting off working on my 10-page report"; Defense: "Once I get started, I will feel better"; Action: "reread the syllabus and spend 15 minutes planning what to write"). Both tables are images in the EPUB (Images/1.png, 2.png); the second example row in 7.3 is cut off at the image edge (see gaps.md). The text also describes a simpler version: a line down a sheet or envelope back.

**D. Method acting / "fake it until you make it"** (Ch 7, §Method Acting Approach): act as if you were someone for whom the behavior is natural (e.g., writing in the Planner before leaving a meeting); pick a role model; script phrases ("Let me take a moment to make sure I write down our next meeting").

**E. Values and Commitment** (Ch 7, §Values and Commitment): eight "slipping habit" questions and eight "why it matters" questions (bullets), used when "coping drift" starts.

**F. Take Away 7.4, Recommitting to Your Plan** (same section):
1. What plan are you struggling with? 2. What interfered? 3. Is it still worth it; have circumstances changed? 4. Make an informed decision by weighing risks/benefits; you may let it go. 5. If keeping it, why is it important? 6. Long-run benefit. 7. How would you feel abandoning vs. continuing vs. completing? 8. Willing to tolerate discomfort? 9. Next steps. 10. Additional resources or help needed.

**G. Downward arrow** (Burns, 1989) (Ch 7, §Changing Your View of Yourself): start from a distorted thought; repeatedly ask "If I assume this is true, what does it say about me?" until reaching the core belief. Worked example ends in "Failure schema."

**H. Perspective taking, gratitude, and reverse-engineering anger back to the trigger** (Ch 7, §Changing Your Thoughts).

## 3. Tools / containers — one job each
- **Thought record form (Table 7.2; or a line down a sheet / envelope back):** externalizes Prosecutor vs. Defense Attorney so thoughts look less convincing than in the head (Ch 7, §Changing Your Thoughts).
- **Table 7.1 Thinking Errors list:** names distortions (magnification/minimization, comparative thinking, all-or-nothing, awfulizing, mind reading, anticipating the future, emotional reasoning, overgeneralization, fallacy of fairness, should statements, jumping to conclusions, labeling, magical thinking/positive bias, externalizing blame, selective abstraction).
- **Table 7.4 Maladaptive Schema list:** self-mistrust, failure, incompetence/inadequacy, defectiveness, instability, unlovability/social exclusion.
- **Daily Planner:** mentioned as the thing to write in at meetings during method-acting practice (Ch 7).

## 7. Key terms (author's words)
- **Invisible fences** — past "shocks" of ADHD frustration condition you to avoid opportunities, even without the collar (Ch 7, opening).
- **Automatic thoughts** — "reflexive thoughts" just out of awareness that can be distorted (Ch 7).
- **Distorted thoughts / thinking errors** — "incorrect or rash conclusions… without conclusive evidence" (Ch 7; Table 7.1).
- **Positive illusory bias** — distorted positive thinking seen in ADHD (Ch 7).
- **Comparative thinking** — judging yourself against others (Ch 7, Table 7.1).
- **Defense Attorney** — your personal advocate who objects to one-sided negative evidence (Ch 7).
- **Method acting approach** — acting as if you are someone for whom the behavior is natural (Ch 7).
- **Coping drift** — slipping from a good start via small breaches (Ch 7, §Values and Commitment).
- **"It doesn't have to be fun to be fun"** — Mark Twight via Ralston (Ch 7).
- **Core beliefs / schema** — enduring "rules for life" formed from childhood on (Beck, 1976; Young & Klosko, 1994) (Ch 7).
- **Downward arrow** — questioning technique that reveals core beliefs (Burns, 1989) (Ch 7).
- **Self-esteem rooted in confidence and competence** — built through facing realistic challenges (Ch 7).
